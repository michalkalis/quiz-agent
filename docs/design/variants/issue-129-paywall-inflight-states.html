Design variants · founder review

Issue #129 — Paywall purchase-in-flight states

Today the paywall has one global busy flag, so buying the one-time pack spins and freezes the pink Subscribe CTA while the Annual card keeps its pink check — the screen says "you are subscribing" during a purchase that is not a subscription. These three variants give three genuinely different answers to the same question: while one product is being bought, what happens to everything else on this screen?

Each variant is a complete, self-consistent position — it answers all five design questions in the issue plus both founder decisions. Pick one whole variant, not a mix; the chosen one goes into Pencil frame z8TS6, then into code.

Rendered against the live dark-mode tokens in Theme+Hangs.swift and the real copy in PaywallView.swift. Prices are RevenueCat displayPrice (locale-formatted at runtime) — shown here in EUR; the founder's field test showed 699,00 Kč / 49,00 Kč.

Reference · shipped buildToday, and why it reads wrong

The current screen during a pack purchase. Two spinners, and the pink check still sits on Annual.

What the screenshot shows

StoreManager.isLoading is one app-wide flag. Every purchase — pack or subscription — flips it, and the CTA passes it straight into HangsPrimaryButton.isLoading, which both spins and disables. The same flag greys the pack row during a subscription buy, and restorePurchases() sets it too.

Meanwhile selectedPlan is plain view state that only plan taps ever change, so there is no way to express "a non-plan product is in flight". The pink check therefore stays put.

"When buying a pack, the Subscribe button shows a loading state. That's weird. And at the same time the subscription is selected even though I'm buying the pack."

Variant AOne Job At A Time

The busy indicator lives only on the thing being bought, and the plan picker openly stands down: both cards deselect, a mono line names what is actually in flight, and the Subscribe CTA stays full-strength and tappable because you genuinely are not subscribing right now.

The five questions, answered

Subscribe CTA during a pack buyUntouched — full pink, tappable. Never spins for someone else's purchase.
One busy indicator or twoAlways exactly one, on the row that owns the purchase.
Pack row during a subscription buyDimmed with a reason line ("Available right after this") — legible as queued, not broken.
Fate of the pink checkDeselects fully; replaced by a mono status line naming what is in flight, so the picker never reads empty.
Restore purchasesSpinner moves onto the link; CTA untouched.
Pack row: peer or escape hatchPeer. Same card weight as the plans; it can host the primary busy state.

Tradeoffs

  • What's boughtclearOne spinner, one active border, one status line — all pointing at the same row.
  • Dead CTA risknoneThe CTA is never disabled by another product's purchase.
  • Double purchaserealA live CTA during a pack buy lets a second StoreKit sheet be requested. StoreManager.purchase() has no in-flight guard today — this variant requires adding one.
  • ImplementationmediumPer-product CTA state, a third "nothing selected" case for the picker, restore-of-selection on resolve, a reentrancy guard, plus three status strings.
  • Founder decisions1 → fully interactive. 2 → yes, deselect.

Variant BSpotlight

Nothing on the screen changes shape. A scrim drops over the whole paywall and only the row being purchased stays lit, floated above it with an explicit sentence. The contradiction disappears because the frozen plan check is literally in the dark.

The five questions, answered

Subscribe CTA during a pack buyRecedes under the scrim at full colour — not restyled, not disabled-looking, just clearly not in front.
One busy indicator or twoOne, always on the lifted card.
Pack row during a subscription buyBehind the scrim like everything else, with "still here when this finishes" — the greyed/broken ambiguity is gone entirely.
Fate of the pink checkStays exactly as it was, dimmed by the scrim. No new selection state to model or restore.
Restore purchasesSame scrim, smaller blue card, no product named.
Pack row: peer or escape hatchStays a secondary escape hatch in the resting layout, but becomes the hero of its own purchase.

Tradeoffs

  • What's boughtunambiguousThe only lit, readable element on the screen names the product and its price in a sentence.
  • Dead CTA risknoneNothing looks disabled; the whole screen is visibly behind a temporary layer.
  • Double purchaseimpossibleThe scrim eats every tap — no reentrancy guard strictly required.
  • ImplementationlargestA new overlay layer with per-product content, an anchored/lifted copy of a row, plus a scrim tap-blocker. But zero changes to selection state or the CTA's own logic.
  • Watch outStoreKit already shows its own sheet over this — during the sheet the founder sees a scrim behind a sheet. Strongest at the moment the sheet is dismissed and the purchase is still settling.
  • Founder decisions1 → non-interactive, but by scrim, not by a dead button. 2 → no, keep the check.

Variant CThe Button Narrates

One status surface for the whole screen: the primary CTA. It morphs into whatever is actually happening — purple "Buying 100 Question Pack" for the pack, pink for a subscription, blue for restore — with a progress track and the product spelled out. No row ever spins.

The five questions, answered

Subscribe CTA during a pack buyBecomes the pack's progress bar — purple, named, non-tappable. It is never a "loading Subscribe button" because it is no longer a Subscribe button.
One busy indicator or twoOne, always in the same place regardless of product — the eye learns where to look.
Pack row during a subscription buyDims to 24% together with the non-selected plan; uniform recession instead of one lonely greyed row.
Fate of the pink checkKept but demoted — hollow pink outline on a 24% card. The CTA copy carries the truth, so the check cannot mislead.
Restore purchasesSame CTA surface, blue; the link fades in place.
Pack row: peer or escape hatchClearly secondary — smaller type and pill by design, since it never has to host a busy state.

Tradeoffs

  • What's boughtstated in wordsThe product name is spelled out on the largest element on screen — the strongest of the three at defeating the founder's exact misread.
  • Dead CTA risklowIt is never a greyed-out button, but it does become non-tappable while narrating. A user who wants to switch to Subscribe mid-pack must wait.
  • Double purchasepreventedThe one control that could start a second purchase is the one occupied by the status.
  • ImplementationsmallestMostly one component: a CTA that takes an in-flight product (label, tint, track) instead of a bare bool. No overlay, no new picker state — only a dim rule and a hollow-check style.
  • Watch outThree CTA tints in one screen risks colour soup, and the label must not wrap — "Buying 100 Question Pack…" needs the single-line + minimum-scale treatment the hero texts already use.
  • Founder decisions1 → non-interactive, repurposed rather than disabled. 2 → keep the check, but demote it.
