Family quiz questions

Questions that kids and grandparents can both have a go at. For birthdays, holidays and Sunday lunch.

20 questions. One person reads, the others guess. Tap to see the answer.

  1. The toy brand name LEGO is squeezed together from two Danish words meaning what?

    Show answer

    Play well

    Founder Ole Kirk Christiansen took it from the Danish phrase 'leg godt', which simply means 'play well'.

    Source: lego.com

  2. The puzzle cube invented by architecture professor Ernő Rubik in 1974 came from which country?

    Show answer

    Hungary

    Rubik built it in Budapest as a teaching aid for spatial thinking and reportedly took a month to solve his own creation.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  3. On a standard six-sided die, the numbers on any two opposite faces always add up to what?

    Show answer

    Seven

    One sits opposite six, two opposite five and three opposite four, so every pair of opposite faces totals seven.

    Source: mathworld.wolfram.com

  4. The Statue of Liberty is famously green, but it didn't start out that way. What colour was it when it was new?

    Show answer

    Shiny copper brown

    Her skin is made of copper, so she began the colour of a new penny. Decades of weather turned her green, and that green layer now protects the metal underneath.

    Source: snopes.com

  5. Which large African animal spends its days half-submerged in rivers and oozes a reddish fluid from its skin that works as its own natural sunscreen?

    Show answer

    Hippopotamus

    Hippos secrete a red-orange liquid, long mistaken for 'blood sweat', whose pigments absorb ultraviolet light and may even help fight germs. It is a built-in sunscreen for an animal with almost no hair.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  6. When you mix baking soda with vinegar and it fizzes up dramatically, which gas is being released in all those bubbles?

    Show answer

    Carbon dioxide

    The acid in vinegar reacts with sodium bicarbonate to release carbon dioxide, the very same gas that makes cakes and bread rise in the oven.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  7. Count the seconds between a lightning flash and its thunder, then divide by three. What does the result tell you?

    Show answer

    Distance to the lightning in kilometres

    Light reaches you almost instantly, but sound travels at about 340 metres per second, so it needs roughly three seconds to cover one kilometre. Dividing the delay by three gives the distance to the strike in kilometres. Dividing by five gives it in miles.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  8. A wingsuit has fabric stretched between the arms and legs, copying a small gliding mammal. Which animal gives the suit its popular nickname?

    Show answer

    Flying squirrel

    Flying squirrels glide by spreading a furry skin flap between their wrists and ankles, and wingsuits copy that design so closely that pilots call them squirrel suits. One leading wingsuit brand is even named Squirrel.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  9. Australia's lyrebird is such a brilliant mimic that it has been recorded copying the sound of which loud power tool heard in its forest?

    Show answer

    Chainsaw

    Lyrebirds weave whatever they hear into their courtship songs, and in forests near logging that includes a near-perfect chainsaw. They've also been heard copying camera shutters and car alarms.

    Source: a-z-animals.com

  10. Some birdseed is coated in hot chilli powder. The birds can't feel the heat at all, but one furry garden raider hates it. Which animal is the chilli meant to keep away?

    Show answer

    Squirrels

    Mammals have a pain receptor that capsaicin switches on, but the bird version simply ignores it. That's probably no accident: birds swallow chilli seeds whole and spread them far and wide.

    Source: audubon.org

  11. Geocachers have a nickname, borrowed from a famous series of children's books, for ordinary passers-by who know nothing about the game and might stumble on a hidden cache. What is it?

    Show answer

    Muggles

    Like Harry Potter's wizards, geocachers try to keep their world hidden from non-magical folk. A cache that a curious stranger finds and throws away is said to have been 'muggled'.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  12. Long before pumpkins, people in Ireland and Scotland carved Halloween lanterns out of a different vegetable. Which one?

    Show answer

    Turnip

    Hollowed-out turnips with scary faces were the first jack-o'-lanterns. Irish and Scottish emigrants in America switched to pumpkins because they were bigger and far easier to carve.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  13. Monopoly grew out of an older board game that was invented as a warning. What was it meant to warn players about?

    Show answer

    Greedy landlords

    Its creator, Lizzie Magie, wanted players to feel how rent makes landowners rich and leaves everyone else broke. The way every Monopoly game ends was the lesson, not just the fun.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  14. The chess word 'checkmate' comes from Persian. Which chess piece is named in its first half?

    Show answer

    The king

    'Shah mat' is Persian for 'the king is helpless'. So every time you say 'check', you are really saying 'king', and the word 'chess' itself comes from the same root.

    Source: etymonline.com

  15. A bungee cord is not really a rope. Its core is a thick bundle of hundreds of thin strands of which stretchy material?

    Show answer

    Rubber

    The cord is basically a huge bundle of rubber bands, so it can stretch to several times its resting length. That stretch spreads the stop over many metres, which is why it feels like a bounce instead of a jolt.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  16. Why does a street sound so strangely hushed right after a fresh, fluffy snowfall?

    Show answer

    Snow absorbs sound

    Fresh snow is mostly air trapped between ice crystals, so it soaks up sound like the foam on a recording studio wall. Once it melts and refreezes into a hard crust, it reflects sound and the hush is gone.

    Source: accuweather.com

  17. When we 'propose a toast', the name comes from something people once actually dropped into their wine or ale. What was it?

    Show answer

    A piece of toasted bread

    In earlier centuries a slice of spiced toast was floated in a drink to add flavour, and possibly to soak up some of the sediment. The drink honouring someone took its name from that soggy slice.

    Source: etymonline.com

  18. After six months floating on the International Space Station, astronauts return to Earth with up to five extra centimetres of something they quickly lose again. Five centimetres of what?

    Show answer

    Height

    Without gravity squashing the spine, the discs between the vertebrae expand and astronauts stretch out, until Earth's pull compresses them back to normal within weeks.

    Source: nasa.gov

  19. A housewarming party was once exactly what it sounds like. Before central heating, what did guests traditionally bring to the new home?

    Show answer

    Firewood

    Guests brought wood and lit fires in every hearth of the empty house, both to warm it and to drive out the evil spirits thought to lurk there. A bottle of wine is the modern stand-in for that armful of logs.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  20. The shiny glaze on a coffee mug isn't paint or varnish. When it melts onto the clay in a hot kiln, it becomes a thin layer of what familiar material?

    Show answer

    Glass

    Glaze is mostly silica, the same stuff as window glass, and in the kiln it melts and fuses to the clay. That's why a glazed mug is waterproof and can be cleaned so easily.

    Source: canada.ca
