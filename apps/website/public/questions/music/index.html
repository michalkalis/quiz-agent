Songs, bands, instruments and the stories behind the hits.

  1. Paul McCartney had the tune for 'Yesterday' long before the words. Until the real lyrics came, he sang it under a placeholder title named after a breakfast dish. What was that title?

    Show answer

    Scrambled Eggs

    McCartney dreamed the melody and, fearing he'd stolen it, sang 'Scrambled eggs, oh my baby how I love your legs' for months until 'Yesterday' finally arrived.

    Source: esquiremag.ph

  2. Michael Jackson's 1983 hit 'Beat It' features a blistering guitar solo by a famous rock guitarist who was not credited on the album and reportedly did it for free. Who was he?

    Show answer

    Eddie Van Halen

    Producer Quincy Jones invited him in, and he recorded the solo in a couple of takes while his own band was out of town, asking nothing in return.

    Source: etonline.com

  3. Nirvana's breakthrough hit 'Smells Like Teen Spirit' got its title from a phrase a friend scrawled on Kurt Cobain's wall. Without Cobain realising it, the phrase referred to a brand of which everyday product?

    Show answer

    Deodorant

    Kathleen Hanna wrote that Kurt smelled like Teen Spirit, the deodorant his girlfriend wore, and Cobain took it as a revolutionary slogan.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  4. In December 1965, two Gemini 6 astronauts smuggled a harmonica and sleigh bells aboard and played the first musical instruments in space. Which song did they perform?

    Show answer

    Jingle Bells

    Wally Schirra and Tom Stafford first radioed mission control about a mysterious 'object' on a polar trajectory, piloted, they said, by someone in a red suit, then broke into 'Jingle Bells' as a Christmas joke.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  5. When MTV went on air for the very first time in August 1981, it deliberately opened with a Buggles song whose title predicted exactly what the channel would do to an older medium. What was the song?

    Show answer

    Video Killed the Radio Star

    MTV chose it as a cheeky mission statement, the very first clip on the first music-video channel declared that video had killed the radio star.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  6. Whitney Houston's giant 1992 ballad 'I Will Always Love You' was written and first recorded in the early 1970s by which country singer?

    Show answer

    Dolly Parton

    Parton wrote it as a farewell to her mentor Porter Wagoner, and Houston's version for The Bodyguard turned it into one of the best-selling singles ever.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  7. For Live Aid in 1985, David Bowie recorded a UK chart-topping duet cover of 'Dancing in the Street' with which fellow rock legend?

    Show answer

    Mick Jagger

    The video premiered at Live Aid and the single topped the UK chart while reaching number seven in the US; the two stars were actually close friends for over four decades.

    Source: grunge.com

  8. Which rock and roll legend had a stillborn identical twin, born 35 minutes before him, whom he later called his original bodyguard?

    Show answer

    Elvis Presley

    Elvis believed his twin brother Jesse was a spiritual guide throughout his life.

    Source: factinate.com

  9. The word 'piano' is short for a longer Italian name that boasted the instrument could do something a harpsichord couldn't: play both soft and what?

    Show answer

    Loud

    Its full name, 'pianoforte', literally means 'soft-loud', because pressing the keys harder made it louder. A harpsichord plucks its strings at the same volume however hard you press.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  10. If you blow into a kazoo the way you'd blow a whistle, almost nothing happens. What do you actually have to do into it to make it buzz?

    Show answer

    Hum

    A kazoo only buzzes when you hum or sing into it, because your voice makes its thin membrane vibrate. So the tune is really coming from you, and the kazoo just adds the buzz.

    Source: madehow.com

  11. A famous punk zine drew three guitar chords and wrote: 'This is a chord, this is another, this is a third.' What did it tell the reader to do next?

    Show answer

    Form a band

    That was the whole lesson: three chords and you're ready to start a band. It became the slogan for punk's do-it-yourself attitude.

    Source: tumblr.austinkleon.com

  12. David Bowie's eyes famously looked like two different colours. What actually caused this illusion?

    Show answer

    A permanently dilated pupil

    His eyeball was scratched in a fight with a friend, leaving one pupil permanently dilated. Both eyes are actually the same colour.

    Source: factinate.com

  13. Singer Ronnie James Dio made the 'metal horns' hand sign famous at concerts. He said he learned it from his Italian grandmother, who used it to protect herself from what?

    Show answer

    The evil eye

    In Italy the 'corna' gesture has long been a charm against the malocchio, the evil eye. Dio simply carried his grandmother's superstition onto the stage, and a whole genre copied him.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  14. In the word 'karaoke', the Japanese 'kara' means 'empty'. The 'oke' part is a clipped borrowing of which English word?

    Show answer

    Orchestra

    Karaoke literally means 'empty orchestra'. The band is all there on the backing track, and the only thing missing is the singer, which is you.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  15. Researchers at the University of Reading found a cheap, everyday trick that helps get rid of a song stuck in your head. It involves keeping your mouth busy. What is it?

    Show answer

    Chewing gum

    Chewing keeps busy the same jaw and 'inner voice' machinery you use to silently sing along. That makes it harder for your brain to keep replaying the tune.

    Source: reading.ac.uk

  16. For decades, nearly every club DJ booth had a pair of Technics turntables. Technics is a brand of which big Japanese electronics company, better known for TVs and household appliances?

    Show answer

    Panasonic

    Technics belongs to Panasonic, formerly called Matsushita. Its SL-1200 was first sold as a home hi-fi deck in the 1970s, before DJs adopted it and turned it into the world's most famous DJ turntable.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  17. Which Scottish band grew so sick of their own 1994 mega-hit 'Love Is All Around', the theme from Four Weddings and a Funeral, that after 15 weeks at UK Number One they deliberately stopped producing the single?

    Show answer

    Wet Wet Wet

    After fifteen consecutive weeks on top, the band deleted the single themselves to halt its run, in the CD era you could actually stop pressing a hit to push fans toward the album instead.

    Source: officialcharts.com

  18. PSY's 2012 horse-dance hit 'Gangnam Style' was the first YouTube video to reach a billion views. Gangnam is a wealthy district of which city?

    Show answer

    Seoul

    The song pokes fun at the flashy lifestyle of Seoul's richest neighbourhood. It was the moment K-pop went truly global.

    Source: blog.youtube

  19. The song often called the very first goth rock record announces the death of the actor best known for playing which famous vampire?

    Show answer

    Dracula

    The 1979 Bauhaus single is 'Bela Lugosi's Dead', a tribute to the Hungarian-born star of the 1931 film Dracula. Goth music was born mourning a movie vampire.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  20. The London club night often called the birthplace of goth, opened in 1982, was named after which superhero's secret hideout?

    Show answer

    Batman

    It was called the Batcave, and its regulars, with their big black hair and pale makeup, became the template for goth club style around the world.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org
