  1. In the classic back-seat game where you playfully punch your neighbour the moment you spot a certain car, which car model are you looking for?

    Show answer

    Volkswagen Beetle

    The game is called Punch Buggy or Slug Bug because the Beetle was nicknamed the 'Bug'. Its rounded shape was unmistakable, and millions were on the road, so there was always someone to hit.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  2. In the classic trucker radio phrase 'ten-four, good buddy', what does 'ten-four' actually mean?

    Show answer

    Message received

    'Ten-four' is one of the 'ten-codes' invented for police radio in the 1930s to keep crackly transmissions short. Truckers adopted the codes, and the CB craze of the 1970s made 'ten-four' famous worldwide.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  3. Truckers on CB radio call highway police 'bears'. The nickname comes from the troopers' wide-brimmed hats, which look like the one worn by which famous cartoon mascot who warns about forest fires?

    Show answer

    Smokey Bear

    American state troopers often wear the same flat-brimmed campaign hat as Smokey Bear, so to truckers a patrol car became a 'bear', and a police officer hiding with a speed gun became a 'bear in the air' when the officer was in a plane.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  4. One red dashboard warning light looks like a little genie's lamp with a single drop falling from its spout. What object is it really a picture of?

    Show answer

    An oil can

    It's an old-fashioned oil can, and it means your engine has lost oil pressure. Unlike most lights, this one means pull over and switch off the engine straight away.

    Source: library.peoplespharmacy.com

  5. On the first frosty morning of the year, your tyre pressure warning light comes on, yet none of your tyres has a puncture. What has switched it on?

    Show answer

    The cold air

    Cold air contracts, so tyre pressure drops by roughly 0.1 bar for every 10 °C fall in temperature, which can be enough to trip the sensor. The light often goes off again once driving warms the tyres up, but it's still worth topping them up.

    Source: clickondetroit.com

  6. Reading a book in a moving car can make you feel sick because your eyes say you're sitting still, while another part of your body says you're moving. Which part?

    Show answer

    Inner ear

    The balance organs deep in your inner ear feel every bump and turn, and when your eyes disagree, the brain gets confused and you feel sick. Looking out at the horizon lets your eyes confirm what your ears already know.

    Source: scientificamerican.com

  7. The word 'nausea' comes from an Ancient Greek word for a means of transport. Which one?

    Show answer

    Ship

    The Greek 'naus' meant ship, the same root as 'nautical', so nausea literally means seasickness. Sailors had the feeling long before anyone got carsick.

    Source: en.wiktionary.org

  8. The car brand Volvo is a real word in Latin. What does it mean?

    Show answer

    I roll

    Volvo is Latin for 'I roll'. It's the same root as 'revolve', and the name first belonged to a ball-bearing company before it ended up on cars.

    Source: www2020.theautochannel.com

  9. The classic Volkswagen hippie bus, the original camper-van icon, was still being built brand-new until 2013. In which country?

    Show answer

    Brazil

    Brazil's Kombi outlived German production by decades and was only stopped when new laws made airbags and anti-lock brakes compulsory, which the old design could not take.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  10. Cars from one European country carry the oval sticker 'CH', which comes from the country's official name in Latin. Which country is it?

    Show answer

    Switzerland

    CH stands for Confoederatio Helvetica, the Swiss Confederation. Switzerland chose Latin so it wouldn't have to pick between German, French, Italian and Romansh, and you'll see the same CH in its web addresses ending in .ch.

    Source: carvolution.com

  11. The Italian surname Ferrari, now famous for sports cars, originally described a man's trade. Which trade?

    Show answer

    Blacksmith

    Ferrari comes from 'ferro', Italian for iron, so the world's most glamorous car brand is really Mr. Smith. It's the Italian cousin of Schmidt, Kovář and Kowalski.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  12. High heels weren't invented for fashion. They began as practical footwear for men doing which activity?

    Show answer

    Horse riding

    Persian cavalrymen wore heels so their feet would stay locked in the stirrups while they stood up to shoot arrows. European noblemen later copied the look, and only after that did heels become a women's fashion.

    Source: today.com

  13. The word 'gym' comes from an ancient Greek word describing how athletes trained. What did that word mean?

    Show answer

    Naked

    'Gymnasium' comes from the Greek 'gymnos', meaning naked, because Greek athletes exercised in the nude. A dress code your local gym has firmly abandoned.

    Source: etymonline.com

  14. When someone fakes sadness, we say they're crying the tears of which animal?

    Show answer

    Crocodile

    Medieval travellers claimed crocodiles weep while devouring their victims. Scientists later filmed alligators and caimans eating and found their eyes really do water, though from the effort of chewing, not regret.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  15. When you bang your 'funny bone', the tingling shoots down into your hand. Which finger usually feels it most?

    Show answer

    Little finger

    The funny bone isn't a bone at all. It's the ulnar nerve, lying almost bare at the elbow, and that nerve supplies your little finger and half of your ring finger.

    Source: assh.org

  16. Which night-singing insect works as a living thermometer, because it chirps faster the warmer the evening gets?

    Show answer

    Cricket

    Crickets are cold-blooded, so their muscles speed up as the air warms. Count the chirps of a snowy tree cricket in 25 seconds, divide by three, add four, and you get roughly the temperature in Celsius.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  17. When a frightened cat puffs its tail up like a bottle brush, tiny muscles are pulling its hairs upright. The very same reflex happens to you. What do you call it when it happens on your skin?

    Show answer

    Goosebumps

    In furry animals this reflex makes them look bigger to a threat and traps warm air in the fur. We've lost most of our fur, so all we get is a skin full of little bumps, which is why fear and cold both give you goosebumps.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  18. When France wins a big match, fans sometimes shout 'Cocorico!' It's the French version of which animal's call, an animal that is also a national symbol of France?

    Show answer

    Rooster

    Cocorico is how a French rooster crows, and since the Gallic rooster is a national emblem, crowing like one has become a way for the French to celebrate.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  19. The 'lady' in the name ladybird, or ladybug, honours one specific woman. Who is she?

    Show answer

    The Virgin Mary

    Medieval farmers called it 'Our Lady's bird' and linked its red wings to Mary's cloak and its seven spots to her seven sorrows. German still calls it Marienkäfer, 'Mary's beetle'.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  20. When your cat ignores a slice of cake, it isn't being polite. Which of the basic tastes can cats not detect at all?

    Show answer

    Sweet

    Cats carry a broken copy of the gene for the sweet-taste receptor, so sugar tastes of nothing to them. A pure meat-eater never needed that sense.

    Source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov
