# Generation Pipeline — Published Best Practices (2026-08)

Scope: external evidence for six design questions about our question-generation pipeline (fact sourcing → generation → critique + pairwise selection → fact verification → 7-dimension gate with 2 judges → embedding dedup). Sources gathered via web search, August 2026; preference for 2024–2026. Two sources are community/informal and marked inline.

---

## 1. LLM-as-judge design

**Per-dimension calls vs one merged rubric call.** Decomposition is the majority practice and the evidence favours it. G-Eval scores aspects independently rather than emitting one blended number ([Confident AI, 2023](https://www.confident-ai.com/blog/g-eval-the-definitive-guide)); FLASK splits coarse scoring into 12 skill-level per-instance scores and reports higher human correlation from the fine-grained form ([FLASK, ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.10928)); HelpSteer rates correctness, coherence, complexity and verbosity separately *specifically* so a merged score cannot let length artifacts leak into the signal ([HelpSteer, NAACL 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09528)). A 2026 study across 20 models found multi-dimensional decomposition cuts self-preference bias by **31.5% on average** vs single-pass combined judging, and that stronger models are *not* less biased ([Quantifying and Mitigating Self-Preference Bias, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22891)).

**Halo is reduced, not eliminated.** Separate-dimension prompting improves judge-to-judge agreement, but cross-dimension score correlation stays elevated either way ([Rubric-Based Evals survey, 2025](https://medium.com/@adnanmasood/rubric-based-evals-llm-as-a-judge-methodologies-and-empirical-validation-in-domain-context-71936b989e80)).

**Pointwise vs pairwise.** Pairwise correlates better with human preference and is more stable, since absolute scoring demands a consistent internal scale. Pointwise is more robust to "distracted evaluation" — a generator drifting toward spurious features the judge likes. Recommendation is to match protocol to purpose, not pick one globally ([Pairwise or Pointwise?, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14716)).

**Judge ensembles.** A Panel of LLM evaluators — three *smaller* judges from disjoint families, combined by max-vote or average — beats a single large judge, shows less intra-model bias, and costs **>7x less** ([Replacing Judges with Juries, Cohere 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18796)). The gain comes from family disjointness, not judge size.

**Biases, quantified.** Position: GPT-4 preferred the first-shown answer in ~60–70% of comparisons whose verdict flipped on swap ([MT-Bench, 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685)), typically a 10–15 winrate-point swing; verbosity inflates preference for longer answers by 15–30 points with no content gain; self-preference runs 10–25% ([Future AGI, 2026](https://futureagi.com/blog/evaluating-llm-judge-bias-mitigation-2026/)). Style bias (markdown over plain prose) was found to be the *dominant* bias at 0.10–0.76 across models vs position bias at ≤0.04 ([Judging the Judges, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23178)). Same-family favouritism is real and traced to shared synthetic-data lineage, not identical weights ([Preference Leakage, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01534)).

**Mitigations that work.** Swap order and count a win only if it holds both ways, else tie; use reference-guided grading where the judge solves the item first or receives a reference answer ([MT-Bench, 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685); [Position Bias in LLM Judges, 2025](https://mbrenndoerfer.com/writing/position-bias-in-llm-judges)). Combined debiasing bought **+4.5 to +11.5 points** of human agreement; best config reached 71.0% agreement (kappa 0.549) at ~$0.001/eval ([Judging the Judges, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23178)).

**Calibrating against humans.** Aligning a judge on a handful of human-labelled examples improves agreement **30–50%** over a generic judge; measure with percent-agreement plus Cohen's kappa ([Databricks/MLflow, 2026](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/databricks/mlflow3/genai/eval-monitor/align-judges)). MemAlign shows a handful of *natural-language feedback* examples can match prompt-optimised judges at lower cost ([Databricks, 2026](https://www.databricks.com/blog/memalign-building-better-llm-judges-human-feedback-scalable-memory)). Hamel Husain warns raw agreement is misleading under class imbalance (track per-class precision/recall) and reports a production judge reaching >90% agreement after **3 iterations** of criteria refinement — criteria are discovered by grading, not specified up front ([Hamel, 2025](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/llm-judge/)). Judges can be calibrated toward human judgment but never fully replace ongoing human review ([Eugene Yan, 2024](https://eugeneyan.com/writing/llm-evaluators/)).

**Implication for our pipeline:** Keep one-dimension-per-call and keep pointwise-for-gate + pairwise-for-selection (both match the evidence), but the two judges must be from disjoint families with neither in the generator's family, add order-swap-with-tie on the pairwise step, and calibrate each rubric to human agreement measured as per-class precision/recall rather than raw score match.

## 2. Over-generation (best-of-N) + critique + selection

**Does more sampling help?** Yes, predictably: best-of-N coverage scales log-linearly with N across four orders of magnitude ([Large Language Monkeys, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21787)). But that is *coverage* (did any sample succeed), which converts into delivered quality only if the selector can spot the winner.

**The selector is the ceiling.** BoN accuracy is characterised by the verifier's ROC geometry: a weak verifier caps the gain regardless of N ([ROC-n-reroll, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.12399)). Worse, BoN is **non-monotonic** in N — it improves then degrades past a knee point as sampling exploits the gap between proxy reward and true quality ([Revisiting (Sub)Optimality of Best-of-N, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05739); [Mitigating Reward Hacking for BoN, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04648)). "Raise N" is not free upside.

**Critique-then-revise vs critique-then-select.** Self-Refine reported **+20% average** across 7 tasks from self-critique and revision ([Self-Refine, NeurIPS 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17651)), but the counter-result is strong: intrinsic self-correction *without external feedback* leaves performance flat or worse ([LLMs Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet, 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798)). Critique works when the critic is a genuinely separate signal: CriticGPT's critiques were preferred over human critiques **>80%** of the time, and human+model teams performed **60% better** than humans alone ([OpenAI, 2024](https://cdn.openai.com/llm-critics-help-catch-llm-bugs-paper.pdf)).

**Creative-specific: the ceiling is diversity, not quality.** Stanford's idea-generation study found over-generate-and-rank hits a hard diversity ceiling — as batches accumulate the share of non-duplicate ideas falls and unique-idea count plateaus; blind expert raters still scored LLM ideas *more novel* than human ideas (p<0.05) but weaker on feasibility, and human re-ranking improved feasibility further ([Can LLMs Generate Novel Research Ideas?, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.04109)). Root cause is a post-training artifact: measured creative diversity falls **45.4% (base) → 20.8% (SFT) → 10.8% (DPO)** ([Verbalized Sampling, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01171)); post-training also flattens thematic and stylistic variation in fiction ([Narrative Flattening, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27878)). The same Verbalized Sampling paper gives a training-free fix orthogonal to temperature — ask for several candidates *with* their probabilities and sample from that distribution — reporting **2–3x diversity** gain on creative writing with quality preserved.

**Implication for our pipeline:** 3x over-generation sits in the safe part of the curve so do not chase a higher N; spend effort on verbalized-sampling-style prompting so the 3 candidates are genuinely different rather than three phrasings of one idea, and on selector quality, since our GPT-5.6 selector is the real ceiling on what over-generation can buy.

## 3. Few-shot examples for creative generation

**How many.** Anthropic's current guidance is **3–5 examples**, and stresses they must be *diverse* "so Claude doesn't pick up unintended patterns" ([Claude prompting best practices, 2026](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices)). Google warns that too many examples cause overfitting to the example set ([Gemini prompt design strategies, 2025](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/prompting-strategies)). Many-shot ICL does keep improving on translation-like tasks with diminishing returns ([Many-Shot ICL, NeurIPS 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.11018)), but on judgment-style tasks more can be strictly worse: one write-up documents "few-shot collapse" (Gemini 3 Flash 64% at 4-shot → 33% at 8-shot, back to the zero-shot baseline) ([Few-Shot Collapse, 2026](https://shuntaro-okuma.medium.com/when-more-examples-make-your-llm-worse-discovering-few-shot-collapse-d3c97ff9eb01), informal source), and a homogenisation study measured 67.23% (0-shot) → 53.94% (100-shot) ([Homogenization Problem in LLMs, 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.06116)). OpenAI notes few-shot's value on reasoning models is unclear, with 5-shot *hurting* o1-class models ([Prompting Guide, 2025](https://www.promptingguide.ai/techniques/fewshot)).

**Negative / anti-pattern examples.** They help *when contrastively framed*, not as bare "bad" samples: Contrastive Chain-of-Thought (correct and incorrect shown together as a pair) gave up to **15 points**, while plain invalid demonstrations without the contrastive framing had minimal impact ([Contrastive CoT, 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09277)). Contrastive ICL "significantly outperforms standard few-shot prompting" for steering tone and style ([Customizing LM Responses with Contrastive ICL, AAAI 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.17390)). The risk is real: a negation-rebound effect ("don't think of a white bear") is confirmed in transformers and *intensifies* with longer, more semantically rich descriptions of what not to do ([Don't Think of the White Bear, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.12381v1)). An alternative is to keep negatives out of the prompt and use them only to *select better positives* ([Failures Are the Stepping Stones to Success, 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.23211)).

**Fixed vs rotated.** Copying bias is documented — models lift answers straight from demonstrations instead of learning the pattern ([Mitigating Copy Bias in ICL, EACL Findings 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01288)); small fixed sets induce spurious input-label mappings ([Comparable Demonstrations, 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.07476)); even surface features like length transfer from demonstrations ([Length Biases in ICL, 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.06653)). Dynamic exemplar selection with MMR-style diversity ranking is the standard counter ([Dynamic Exemplar Selection, 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.01466)).

**Annotated exemplars.** Rationale-augmented demonstrations (input → rationale → output) improve both accuracy and interpretability over bare input→output pairs ([Rationale-Augmented Ensembles, 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00747); [Chain-of-Thought, 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.11903)), with the caveat that hand-written rationales are often sub-optimal and can hurt if sloppy ([Synthetic Prompting, 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.00618)).

**Implication for our pipeline:** 4 positive + 3 negative is a defensible count but the *fixed* set is the defect — rotate gold examples per call from a larger diverse pool, pair each anti-pattern with its corrected version (contrastive framing) instead of listing bad examples alone, and attach a one-line "why this is good" rationale to each gold example.

## 4. Model-family-specific prompting

**Prompts do not transfer cleanly.** Prompt migration across model versions is an acknowledged production problem requiring active tooling ([Prompt Migration, 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.05573)); format changes alone swing performance up to 40% in multimodal settings ([Promptception, 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.03986)) and >70% across semantically equivalent prompts in knowledge probing ([Do We Know What LLMs Don't Know?, 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.21701)). Counterweight: much measured "prompt sensitivity" is an artifact of rigid evaluation (log-likelihood scoring, exact match) rather than true model fragility ([Flaw or Artifact?, EMNLP 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.01790)) — so retune, but do not assume every wobble is the model's fault.

**Vendor guidance now differs materially, and in places contradicts.** Anthropic publishes *per-model* prompting pages (Fable 5, Sonnet 5, Opus 5, Opus 4.8) with different response-length, tool-triggering and effort defaults, and warns that older aggressive tool-forcing language causes overtriggering on newer models ([Claude prompting best practices, 2026](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices)). OpenAI prescribes a version-specific migration path with explicit `reasoning_effort` settings per source model ([GPT-5.2 Prompting Guide, 2026](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/gpt-5/gpt-5-2_prompting_guide)). Google tells developers to **stop** hand-writing chain-of-thought scaffolding (correct for Gemini 2.5) and use the `thinking_level` parameter with simplified prompts, and never to set temperature below 1.0 because it can cause looping or degraded output ([Gemini 3 Developer Guide, 2025](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/gemini-3)) — a direct conflict with the manual reasoning-scaffolding style the other two vendors still encourage.

**Structured output and quality.** The canonical result: JSON mode forced an `answer`-before-`reason` key order, collapsing zero-shot CoT into direct answering and causing significant reasoning decline on GSM8K ([Let Me Speak Freely?, EMNLP Industry 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02442)) — though the same paper found the effect task-dependent (Gemini 1.5 Flash *improved* under JSON on a classification task). Follow-ups narrow the claim: once reasoning is decoupled from the structured field, schema-constrained decoding barely moves accuracy (delta −0.007 to +0.033) ([Structured Output Benchmark, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.25359v1)), and a 2026 reframing argues the degradation is a model-capacity problem, not a format problem ([Capacity, Not Format, 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.09410)).

**Thinking modes and creative quality.** Evidence is thin and mixed. A community discussion reports extended reasoning makes creative output "more correct but less spontaneous", with non-thinking mode often preferred for brainstorming and storytelling ([reasoning-switch discussion, 2026](https://huggingface.co/datasets/John6666/forum3/blob/main/creative_writing_reasoning_switch_llm_1.md), informal, no formal citation found); against that, training models to generate thoughts reportedly improved creative writing alongside reasoning (secondary summary only, original paper unverified).

**Implication for our pipeline:** Maintain one prompt variant per model role rather than a shared template — Claude Fable 5 per Anthropic's model page, Gemini 3.1 Pro with simplified prompts + `thinking_level` and temperature ≥1.0 (verify we are not lowering it) — and in every structured-output schema put the reasoning/critique field **before** the score or answer field.

## 5. Trivia / quiz question generation

**What others' pipelines look like.** LLM-based question generation collapses the classic concept → stem → distractors → filter pipeline but introduces hallucinated facts and superficial distractors as the new dominant failure modes ([MCQ agentic pilot study, 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.18891)); in high-stakes domains LLM item generation now reaches expert-comparable ratings ([Automatic Q&A Generation via LLM, 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.19475)). Multi-agent designs assign separate agents to stem, distractors and difficulty ([Cognitively Diverse MCQ Generation, 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.03704)), and Bloom's-taxonomy-controlled generation is evaluated per cognitive level ([Educational QG at Bloom's Levels, 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.04394)).

**Quality dimensions others use.** QGEval is the closest analogue to our gate: **7 dimensions** — fluency, clarity, conciseness, relevance, consistency, answerability, answer consistency — scored over 3k questions from 15 QG models, finding models weakest specifically on **answerability** and **answer consistency** ([QGEval, EMNLP 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.05707)), with earlier dedicated answerability-metric work ([2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.12546v2)).

**Distractors.** Survey standard is plausibility, semantic similarity and homogeneity, with *implausible distractors* the single most common flaw in both human and automated review ([Alhazmi et al., EMNLP 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.799/)). Stronger approaches tie each distractor to a specific represented misconception rather than a random wrong value ([DiVERT, 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.19356)), enforce surface-form consistency in length and style to remove giveaway cues ([LookAlike, 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.01903)), or predict real student choice to estimate diagnostic power ([Student Choice Prediction, 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.13125)); D-GEN packages generation plus an evaluation harness ([2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13439)). The psychometric baseline remains Haladyna & Downing's 31 validated item-writing rules — make distractors plausible, avoid "all of the above", avoid grammatical and length cues ([Haladyna et al., 2002](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1207/S15324818AME1503_5); [rule text](https://cpl.health.unm.edu/AssetListing/Writing-Questions-for-Learning-and-Assessment-JiTL-2573/Rules-for-Multiple-Choice-Items-Haladyna-et-al-2002-5943)).

**Products and datasets.** Duolingo shipped 148 LLM-generated courses in roughly a year vs 12 years for the first 100, with human experts still owning the prompts (fixed rules + variable content parameters), and generates the first question separately from the rest to avoid overloading the model ([ZenML LLMOps DB, 2024/25](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/ai-powered-lesson-generation-system-for-language-learning); [Duolingo Engineering, 2025](https://blog.duolingo.com/unique-engineering-problems/)). Khanmigo generates standards-aligned questions but routes them through a teacher edit step before export ([Khan Academy](https://www.khanmigo.ai/question-generator)). Rearc/Databricks published an end-to-end RAG+LLM quiz pipeline prompt-engineered per stage ([Rearc](https://www.rearc.io/blog/quizzes-with-rag-and-llms-on-databricks)). Dataset precedent: TriviaQA pairs each of 95K human-authored trivia questions with ~6 independently gathered evidence documents ([2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.03551)); HotpotQA annotates sentence-level supporting facts per question ([2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.09600)).

**Implication for our pipeline:** Our 7 dimensions should explicitly cover QGEval's two weakest-in-practice ones — answerability and answer consistency, ideally enforced by round-trip answering rather than a judge opinion — and MCQ distractors need an explicit Haladyna-style structural check (misconception-anchored, length/style-homogeneous, no giveaway cues) that a generic quality rubric will not catch.

## 6. Pipeline completeness — stages we lack

**Revision loop (fix-and-rescore) vs drop.** Conditional, not a blanket win. Naive iterative self-refinement without an external signal often *degrades* output; setups that pay off keep generator and verifier separate ([When Does Verification Pay Off?, 2025](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2512.02304)). Knowing when to stop repairing vs discard is itself a hard sub-problem ([Verify, Repair, Repeat, or Stop?, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17641v1)).

**Difficulty calibration.** Predicting item difficulty from text alone is genuinely hard: the BEA 2024 shared task (real USMLE items, run by NBME) saw best text-based systems reach RMSE 0.165, with difficulty markedly harder to predict than response time ([overview](https://osf.io/dwe4n/); [UnibucLLM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.13343); [ITEC](https://aclanthology.org/2024.bea-1.43/); [scalar-mixed transformer](https://aclanthology.org/2024.bea-1.40/)). Cold-start options: simulate IRT-ability-aligned "students" to pre-calibrate items before deployment ([SMART, 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.05129)), or use LLMs as item-evaluating respondents ([BJET, 2025](https://bera-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/bjet.13570)); IRT difficulty of LLM-generated items has correlated with human-calibrated items at Pearson >0.7 ([2024/25](https://onlinesciencepublishing.com/index.php/ajel/article/view/1543)).

**Retrieval diversity.** MMR (rank by relevance minus redundancy against what is already selected) is the standard fix for clustered or duplicate evidence ([explainer, 2024](https://anton-dergunov.github.io/posts/2024/07/maximal-marginal-relevance)), extended into RAG context selection via relevance-information-gain ranking ([Better RAG using Relevant Information Gain, 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.12101)).

**Topic / curriculum planning.** Persona Hub drives diversity by conditioning generation on 1B auto-curated personas explicitly to avoid clustered output ([2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.20094)); staged Evol-Instruct (complexity evolution) plus Self-Instruct (novel-task generation) is the standard recipe for diversified synthetic corpora ([COT-Self-Instruct, 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.23751)).

**Human-in-the-loop gates.** The systematic-review recommendation is to combine **uncertainty sampling with diversity sampling** when choosing which items get human review, rather than either alone ([HITL systematic review, 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/1099-4300/28/4/377); [active-learning HITL pipelines](https://humansintheloop.org/how-do-you-build-human-in-the-loop-ai-pipelines-using-active-learning/)). Item-generation reviews consistently advise human validation for accuracy and fairness even where LLMs draft items quickly ([D-GEN discussion, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13439)); no source gave a defensible numeric sampling rate.

**Implication for our pipeline:** Highest-value missing stages in order are (a) a bounded targeted revision loop with an explicit stop rule, safe for us because our critic is a different family from the generator, (b) MMR-style diversity on Tavily/Wikipedia fact retrieval plus a topic/persona planner upstream of generation, and (c) a founder review queue populated by uncertainty+diversity sampling rather than by whatever survives the gate.
