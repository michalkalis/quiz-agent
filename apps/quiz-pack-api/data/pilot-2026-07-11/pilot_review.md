**B1. [text]** Why is it nearly impossible to fold a standard sheet of paper in half more than seven times, no matter how hard you try?
   - Answer: **Thickness doubles each fold, width halves**
   - Context: Each fold doubles the number of layers, so the thickness grows exponentially, while the width of the foldable area shrinks by half each time. After about seven folds, the paper is thicker than it is wide — you'd need to bend a stack dozens of layers thick across a strip barely a centimetre wide, which the material simply can't do. This is a geometric constraint, not a strength problem: even very large sheets hit the same wall, just a fold or two later.
