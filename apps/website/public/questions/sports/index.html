Olympics, records and odd moments from stadiums and slopes.

  1. James Naismith's first basketball hoops, nailed to a gym balcony in 1891, were containers originally meant for which fruit?

    Show answer

    Peaches

    Naismith nailed two peach baskets to the balcony railing, and since they still had bottoms, someone had to fetch the ball out after every score.

    Source: history.com

  2. For the first half of the 20th century, you could win an official Olympic medal without breaking a sweat. What kind of competition did you have to enter?

    Show answer

    Art

    From 1912 to 1948 the Olympics awarded medals for sport-inspired painting, sculpture, music, literature and architecture. The founder of the modern Games, Pierre de Coubertin, even won a gold for a poem he entered under a pseudonym.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  3. In the mid-1970s a severe drought hit California, and skateboarders suddenly gained a whole new kind of place to ride. What was it?

    Show answer

    Empty swimming pools

    Water restrictions left backyard pools drained, and skaters carved their curved walls. That is how vertical skating was born, and it eventually led to the half-pipe.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  4. The last Olympic gold medals made of solid gold were handed out in 1912. Ever since, they've been made almost entirely of a different metal, with just a few grams of gold plating on top. Which metal?

    Show answer

    Silver

    Modern Olympic gold medals must be at least 92.5 percent silver, coated with roughly six grams of pure gold, melted down, today's 'gold' is worth far less than its glory suggests.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  5. Football referees show a yellow card for a warning and a red card for a sending-off. Which everyday sight gave a referee the idea for this colour system?

    Show answer

    Traffic lights

    Referee Ken Aston was stopped at a traffic light when he realised it already told everyone 'caution' and 'stop'. That mattered because players and referees from different countries often couldn't understand each other's spoken warnings.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  6. Tennis balls were white for about a hundred years before the official switch to bright yellow. Who was the change mainly meant to help?

    Show answer

    Television viewers

    Studies showed that white balls were hard to follow on colour TV, so the governing body adopted 'optic yellow' to make them easier for viewers to see. Wimbledon held out with white balls until 1986.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  7. Mavericks, California's most feared big-wave spot, is named after one of the first to swim out there. Maverick wasn't a person, though. What was Maverick?

    Show answer

    A dog

    Maverick was a white German Shepherd who swam out after his owner and friends. The wave that has since humbled the world's best surfers still carries the dog's name.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  8. Instead of champagne, what drink does the winner of the Indianapolis 500 traditionally down in victory lane?

    Show answer

    Milk

    The custom began in 1936 when winner Louis Meyer asked for buttermilk, and today drivers even pre-register whether they want whole, two percent or skimmed.

    Source: indianapolismotorspeedway.com

  9. The 'ollie', the basic hop every snowboarder learns first, was invented in a different board sport. Which one?

    Show answer

    Skateboarding

    A Florida skater, Alan 'Ollie' Gelfand, invented the move in the late 1970s and it was named after his nickname. Snowboarders borrowed it, along with much of their trick vocabulary.

    Source: guinnessworldrecords.com

  10. The marathon's oddly precise distance of 42.195 kilometres dates from the 1908 London Olympics, when the course was stretched so the race could start on the lawn of which royal residence?

    Show answer

    Windsor Castle

    The route ran from Windsor Castle to the royal box at White City Stadium, and that royal convenience later became the official marathon distance worldwide.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  11. Which team event, today mostly seen at school sports days and village fairs, awarded Olympic medals at every Games from 1900 to 1920?

    Show answer

    Tug of war

    Teams of eight pulled a rope for Olympic gold, and in 1908 a City of London police team famously won after a dispute about their heavy boots.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  12. After falling out with his brother Adi in the 1940s, Rudolf Dassler founded which sportswear brand, still based in the same small German town as Adidas?

    Show answer

    Puma

    The feud split the town of Herzogenaurach so deeply that locals reportedly checked which shoes a stranger was wearing before speaking to them.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  13. In snooker, what score is a maximum break, achieved by potting every red with a black and then clearing all the colours?

    Show answer

    147

    Fifteen reds paired with fifteen blacks give 120, and the six colours add 27 more, so the perfect frame is worth 147.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  14. On a standard pub dartboard, the 20 sits at the top. Which two low numbers sit right beside it, so a slightly wayward throw is punished?

    Show answer

    1 and 5

    The board's layout is designed to reward accuracy, so a dart that just misses the 20 scores only 1 or 5. Aim a fraction off a treble 20 and you can drop from 60 points to 3.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  15. In darts, the highest finish you can make with three darts is 170. That's two treble twenties and then one more dart. Where does that last dart have to land?

    Show answer

    The bullseye

    Two treble twenties make 120, leaving exactly 50, and the bullseye is worth 50 and counts as a double, so it can legally end the game. Darts fans call this finish 'the big fish'.

    Source: dartcounter.net

  16. Which is the only country to have played at every men's football World Cup since the first one in 1930?

    Show answer

    Brazil

    Brazil has never failed to qualify, while heavyweights like Germany, Italy and Argentina have all missed at least one tournament.

    Source: copaamerica.com

  17. The oldest international trophy in sport, first won in 1851 and still contested today, is awarded in which sport?

    Show answer

    Sailing

    The America's Cup was first won by the yacht America off the Isle of Wight in 1851, decades before the first modern Olympics or the first football World Cup.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  18. Why did the designer of the Olympic symbol choose exactly those colours: blue, yellow, black, green and red rings on a white background?

    Show answer

    Every national flag contains one

    Pierre de Coubertin picked those six colours because every country's flag uses at least one of them, so every nation could see itself in the symbol. Many people think each colour stands for a continent, but no colour was ever officially tied to a particular continent.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  19. The stuntman who skydived dressed as the Queen at the London 2012 Olympics also made the first wingsuit landing without opening a parachute. What did he crash-land onto?

    Show answer

    Cardboard boxes

    Gary Connery glided down from a helicopter and slammed into a runway of about 18,600 empty cardboard boxes, which crumpled one after another to soak up his speed. He walked away unhurt.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  20. Snowboarders who ride with their right foot forward are nicknamed after a Disney character who surfed that way in a 1930s cartoon. Which character?

    Show answer

    Goofy

    In the 1937 short 'Hawaiian Holiday', Goofy rides a surfboard right foot forward, so surfers adopted 'goofy' for that stance and snowboarders kept it. Left foot forward is simply called 'regular'.

    Source: surfertoday.com
