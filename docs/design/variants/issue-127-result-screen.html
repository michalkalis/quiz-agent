Design session · 2026-07-28

Issue #127 — Result screen redesign

The Result screen today leads with a 52pt “MISSED IT.” and buries the two things a wrong answer actually needs — the correct answer and why — below it, while the pinned nav has no visual boundary so the hero clips against it on scroll. All three variants below rebuild the hierarchy rather than repaint it: each one takes a different position on what dominates (verdict / answer card / a fixed no-scroll deck), where the explanation lives, and how much footer chrome survives.

Every mockup uses the real shipped tokens and copy from Theme+Hangs.swift, ResultView.swift and the Hangs component library — real hexes, Anton / IBM Plex Mono / Inter, real button heights, the real CmdListenBar and the #108B countdown CTA. Frames are 390×844 (1:1 with iOS points). Score is never made dominant in any variant — it lost that argument in #84; it appears as a chip, a compact box, or inline with the verdict, and each variant says which.

Content shown is one sample question · dashed pink lines mark where pinned chrome ends and scrolling begins · light appearance shown; every token has a dark-mode pair.

VARIANT A

ANSWER FIRST

The verdict shrinks to the chip it already is, and the answer card becomes the whole screen. On a wrong answer the correct answer is the top row at 44pt and the user's own answer drops to a struck-through footnote — the exact inverse of today. The explanation is always-on inline body copy, so “Why is this correct?” disappears from the footer entirely.

What this variant decides

Dominant elementThe answer card. The verdict is a chip; the score is a header pill. Nothing competes with the 44pt answer.
Correct vs incorrectOne shared layout. The 44pt slot always holds the canonical answer; only the label (“your answer” / “the answer”) and the bottom row (question recap / struck “you said”) swap.
ExplanationInline, always visible, under a blue WHY label, on both states. The web sheet degrades to a mono source · nasa.gov line inside the card.
Pinned / scroll boundaryNav + progress + verdict strip are pinned as one band; the card region scrolls beneath it. Boundary marked by the strip's own padding, so nothing can clip mid-glyph.
Footer inventoryCmdListenBar · “Next question” (with #108B countdown) · “Stay here” (→ “Resume auto-advance” when paused). “Why is this correct?” is cut.
“Try this question again”Formally dropped. The answer is already revealed, so a retry has nothing left to test.

Tradeoffs

  • Glanceability — one word at 44pt is the only large text; a driver's eye lands on the answer with no scan.
  • Wrong-answer hierarchy — best of the three. Correct answer first, explanation second, own answer last, source last.
  • Scroll — the card can still overflow on a long explanation; scrolling is confined to one card, but auto-advance may pull the screen away mid-read.
  • Footer — 2 controls plus the listening bar; the shortest footer of the three.
  • Brand cost — “NAILED IT.” / “MISSED IT.” disappears entirely. The screen loses its most characteristic moment.
VARIANT B

VERDICT HERO, DIVERGENT STATES

Keeps the Anton verdict as the brand moment but pins it and caps it at 40pt so it can never clip or wrap — then lets the two states genuinely diverge. Correct celebrates (full hero, explanation collapsed as a reward you can open). Incorrect teaches (the hero collapses to a 26pt inline strip, and the explanation is expanded by default because that is the moment it matters).

What this variant decides

Dominant elementThe verdict — but only when you were right. On a wrong answer the answer card takes over and the verdict becomes a 26pt inline strip.
Correct vs incorrectGenuinely divergent. Different hero size, different card contents, different default disclosure state. Two layouts to build and maintain.
ExplanationCollapsible row, state-dependent default: closed on correct (a “reward” you opt into), open on incorrect. The full-screen SourceWebView survives behind a ghost “Read the source” — now shown on both states, closing the isCorrect && gate at ResultView.swift:225.
Pinned / scroll boundaryNav + progress + hero all pinned; only cards scroll. The 52pt headline drops to 40pt (correct) / 26pt (incorrect) with a hard one-line rule, which removes the clipping mechanism at its source.
Footer inventoryCorrect: CmdListenBar · “Next question” · “Stay here”. Incorrect adds a ghost “Read the source”. “Why is this correct?” moves out of the footer and into the card as a disclosure row.
“Try this question again”Dropped, and the freed footer slot is spent on the source link instead.

Tradeoffs

  • Glanceability — strongest emotional read on a correct answer; the wrong-answer strip is calm and puts the answer at 36pt.
  • Wrong-answer hierarchy — answer, then why, then your answer. Only marginally behind Variant A, and it keeps the brand voice.
  • Scroll — the tallest content of the three (card + score box + disclosure). Real scrolling is expected, which is a poor fit for a moving car.
  • Footer — up to 4 stacked elements on the incorrect state; the busiest footer here.
  • Build cost — two divergent layouts plus a disclosure animation; the most implementation surface and the most snapshot churn.
VARIANT C

ZERO-SCROLL DECK

The driving-safety maximalist: the screen never scrolls, so the header cannot clip by construction. Three fixed zones — chrome, a colour-washed verdict field that carries the verdict word and the score together, and an answer panel that fills whatever is left. The explanation is capped at about five lines with a hear it button that speaks it, replacing the unusable-in-motion web sheet; the footer consolidates to a single row.

What this variant decides

Dominant elementThe answer at 46pt, inside a panel whose colour carries the verdict. The verdict word survives at 30pt inside the tinted field; the score sits inline next to it and is never a card.
Correct vs incorrectOne layout, byte-identical structure. Only the field tint, the labels and the last row (question recap vs struck “you said”) change. Cheapest to build and to keep in sync.
ExplanationInline, hard-clamped to ~5 lines, with a blue hear it control that speaks the explanation via the existing TTS path. The web sheet is demoted to a mono source line — legal to tap when parked, invisible when driving.
Pinned / scroll boundaryThere is no boundary — nothing scrolls. The panel flexes to the viewport, so the clipping mechanism (#127 finding 3) cannot occur on any screen size.
Footer inventoryCmdListenBar plus one row: a 76pt “STAY” pill next to the “Next question” CTA. Paused state swaps the pause glyph for play — same slot, no extra row. “Why is this correct?” is cut.
“Try this question again”Formally dropped — there is no fixed-height budget for it, and the answer is already on screen.

Tradeoffs

  • Glanceability — highest. Colour tells you the verdict pre-attentively; the answer is the largest thing on screen; nothing moves under your thumb.
  • Wrong-answer hierarchy — answer, why, then a 12pt footnote for what you said. Same as A, plus an audio route to the explanation.
  • Scroll — none. The header-clipping bug is structurally impossible, and auto-advance can never yank content mid-scroll.
  • Footer — the simplest: two controls on one row, fixed height, no state-dependent stacking.
  • Truncation — long explanations lose their tail and long answers must scale down. Content quality has to carry the constraint, or the “hear it” route becomes mandatory rather than optional.
  • Brand cost — the Anton verdict survives, but at 30pt inside a tinted box it is a label, not a moment.

Open questions for the founder that the mockups deliberately do not pre-decide: whether the explanation should appear on correct answers at all (all three variants say yes — symmetry serves learning) · whether an audio “hear it” route is worth the TTS cost per result · and whether the CmdListenBar earns its ~56pt on this screen now that the CTA already carries the countdown.
