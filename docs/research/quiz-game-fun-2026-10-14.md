# What makes trivia fun as a game, and what fits a voice app

**Date:** 2026-10-14 · **Trigger:** founder playtest, "it's simply a boring quiz", while blind question ratings average ~7.3/10.
**Scope:** game-loop levers beyond question quality. Known findings are not repeated here (Kang/Loewenstein curiosity peak, quizbowl stock clues, recognition vs recall).
**Evidence labels:** **data** = controlled study or hard metrics · **postmortem** = maker or press account · **anecdote** = review, forum or opinion.

## TL;DR

Good questions are necessary but not sufficient. Every hit format below adds at least one of these on top of the question: **a decision with something at stake** (Millionaire, The Chase, Pointless, 1% Club), **people to play with or against** (Trivia Crack, QuizUp, Kahoot, Jackbox, Volley), **a personality that reacts** (YDKJ, HQ, QI, Wait Wait), and **a reveal built as a moment** (tease, then surprise). As described, Trubbo's loop has none of the four: one player, flat scoring, a neutral voice, a straight reveal. That's a likely explanation for "boring" despite decent questions (inference, not measured).

## 1. Products: what made them work, what killed them

| Product | Engine of fun | What limited or killed it | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **HQ Trivia** | Live appointment at a fixed time, real cash, elimination after each 10 s question, "savage" questions that wipe out the field, a comedian host ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HQ_(video_game)), [Ypulse](https://www.ypulse.com/article/2018/01/03/behind-the-hq-trivia-hype-talking-to-the-popular-app-its-famous-host/)). "Erasers" were earned by playing with two nearby players, a reward for physical co-presence. | Novelty faded, prize money cost too much, payouts came late, tech glitches, leadership chaos. Installs fell from 2M (Feb 2018) to 67k (Jan 2020) ([The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/hq-trivia-shuts-down-after-3-years), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/02/14/hq-trivia-shuts-down)). Peak 2.38M concurrent players, final games ~15k. | postmortem |
| **QuizUp** | 1v1 on a topic you pick, 7 questions, 10 s each, faster answers score more, last question doubles. Difficulty rises with topic level. A bot replays real players' timings when no human is free (<5% of games) ([PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/444189/you-should-play-quizup.html)). | 70–80M users but too little revenue. A bet on an NBC TV show failed. Sold to Glu, then shut down ([Northstack](https://www.northstack.is/pv-wind-down/), [ProductMint](https://productmint.com/what-happened-to-quizup/)). The game wasn't what failed; the business was. | postmortem |
| **Trivia Crack** | A wheel of chance picks the category, you collect 6 crowns, async play against Facebook friends. Questions are user-submitted and localized per region ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/01/23/millennials-favorite-trivia-game-dominates-app-store-charts), [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/insider/2014/11/27/latin-american-mobile-game-trivia-crack-conquered-us-market/)). | Still alive. Coins buy a second chance or a skip, a soft lifeline. | postmortem |
| **Kahoot** | Shared screen, music under a countdown, points for speed, top-5 leaderboard after each question. A 593-student 2×2 experiment found that turning points and audio on or off made a significant difference to concentration, engagement, enjoyment and motivation. Music had the biggest effect on room dynamics ([Wang & Lieberoth 2016](https://folk.idi.ntnu.no/alfw/publications/ECGBL2016-Effect_of_points_and_audio_in_Kahoot.pdf)). | A review of 93 studies lists time-pressure stress and fear of losing as the main downsides ([Wang & Tahir 2020](https://research.gold.ac.uk/id/eprint/39435)). | data |
| **YDKJ / Jackbox** | Gottlieb asked "how could a trivia game be funny?" and hired Chicago improv writers. A sarcastic host reads offbeat questions that mix high and pop culture. Sold ~5M copies, $100M ([ClickZ](https://www.clickz.com/digital-mad-man-you-dont-know-jack-founder-harry-gottlieb/44666/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You_Don%27t_Know_Jack_(1995_video_game))). In Trivia Murder Party, players knocked out become ghosts who keep playing and can win back their life in the final chase ([GamingTrend](https://gamingtrend.com/reviews/kill-your-friends-betray-your-friends-make-a-new-word-with-your-friends-the-jackbox-party-pack-6/)). | Host jokes repeat within 3 games, which reviewers criticise ([CGM](https://cogconnected.com/preview/trivia-murder-3-preview/)). | postmortem / anecdote |
| **Heads Up** | Charades with one phone: others give you clues, it's physical, and it records shareable clips. 16M downloads ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=811297), [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2015/04/14/hln-ellen-degeneres-heads-up-app-game-show/)). | Ellen's TV exposure did most of the work. | postmortem |
| **Millionaire** | Stakes high enough to matter. The contestant **sees the question and options before deciding** whether to play on. Can walk away any time. Three lifelines, safety-net levels, a thriller-style score and lighting ([Briggs on NPR Fresh Air](https://freshairarchive.org/segments/david-briggs), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Who_Wants_to_Be_a_Millionaire%3F_(British_game_show))). | — | postmortem |
| **The Chase** | A named antagonist (the Chaser). The contestant picks an offer: a risky big prize or a safe small one, even zero or negative ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Chase_(British_game_show))). | — | anecdote (no designer statement found) |
| **Pointless** | Inverted scoring: the less-known correct answer scores better, so the obvious answer is a trap, and viewers play along at home ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pointless), [CPH Post](https://cphpost.dk/2014-01-10/general/tv-listings-pointless-answers-are-essential-viewing/)). | — | anecdote |
| **The 1% Club** | Logic, not knowledge. Each question is labelled by the % of the public who solved it. 15 questions climb from 90% to 1%, one pass, the option to bank. The UK's biggest game show of 2023, now in 15 territories. The makers' reason: "a show that you can play with family… it tested how your brain works, not how much knowledge you have", and it "starts pretty well and then just grows" ([TVBizz](https://tvbizzmagazine.com/making-the-one-percent-club), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_1%25_Club), [Advanced Television](https://advanced-television.com/?p=240125)). | — | postmortem |
| **QI / Only Connect** | QI awards points for *interesting* wrong answers and fires a klaxon at popular misconceptions ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QI)). Only Connect: work out the *type* of link, with red herrings, so educated guessing beats recall ([Den of Geek](https://www.denofgeek.com/?p=839912)). | Niche and cerebral. | anecdote |
| **Wait Wait… Don't Tell Me** (radio, the closest audio analogue) | Game structure plus improv comedy. Varied mini-formats (questions, role-play, limericks). Unscripted panel ([The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/2018/1/4/16849150/wait-wait-dont-tell-me-npr-quiz-show), [Berkeley](https://alumni.berkeley.edu/california-magazine/january-february-2007-25-brilliant-california-ideas/not-your-daddys-npr/)). | Struggled for its first years before the format settled. | postmortem |
| **Pub quiz** | Teams, conferring, a social night out. | No peer-reviewed research found; claims are anecdotal. | anecdote |

## 2. Voice-specific lessons (Volley, Alexa)

- **Retention is the core problem for voice apps.** Only ~3% of users of an Alexa skill were active in week 2 (Jan 2017), rising to ~6% by Sept 2017. Outliers reached 20%+, and habit-forming skills did much better ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2017/01/23/amazon-echo-google-home-voice-app), [Voicebot](https://www.voicebot.ai/2017/09/20/voice-app-retention-doubled-9-months-according-voicelabs-data/)). **data** (industry analytics)
- **Volley's own lessons** (Max Child): (a) live multiplayer is "the coolest thing" voice enables, a social medium that web and mobile weren't; (b) the goal is "hundreds of hours", which takes constant new content (expansion packs, more writers) ([Amazon dev blog 2018](https://developer.amazon.com/en-US/blogs/alexa/alexa-skills-kit/2018/06/in-skill-purchasing-takes-volley-s-thriving-voice-business-to-the-next-level)); (c) trivia, music and licensed game shows were their strongest genres, while free-form chat games "never got more than a couple thousand users"; (d) ~5% speech-recognition errors frustrate players, so constrain what the game listens for; (e) one subscription for everything beats per-game sales, and ads are weak ([Naavik podcast](https://naavik.co/podcast/the-next-era-of-voice-games/)); (f) the hit *Yes Sire* came from "greatly simplifying the interactions" after a failed game ([Alexa Games Week](https://developer.amazon.com/en-US/blogs/alexa/alexa-skills-kit/2020/05/8-things-we-learned-from-alexa-games-week)). **postmortem.** Caveat: one reviewer argues Song Quiz's "opponents" were not real-time humans ([Medium](https://medium.com/voice-games/review-song-quiz-1a565581c4a6), anecdote). Even so, the *feeling* of an opponent was the hook.
- **Question of the Day** (Matchbox.io, bought by Volley for "multi-million") won by a **daily ritual** of one question, short and polite sessions, a deliberately **silly fourth MCQ option** "to add delight", streaks and a city/state leaderboard shared to the phone, plus heavy A/B testing (one word in the upsell raised conversion 14%) ([Amazon dev blog 2022](https://developer.amazon.com/en-US/blogs/alexa/alexa-skills-kit/2022/03/Alexa-innovators-matchbox-question-of-the-day), [Voicebot](https://voicebot.ai/2022/02/23/volley-buys-question-of-the-day-developer-matchbox-io-in-multi-million-dollar-deal/)). **postmortem**
- **Jeopardy on Alexa:** usage spiked right after the TV episode aired (a ritual tied to an external event). "Keep the content fresh" was the main retention lesson. Subscription = extra daily clues plus missed days ([Amazon dev blog](https://developer.amazon.com/blogs/alexa/post/f36e5480-dd21-43fd-b842-a349c9452926/jeopardy-america-s-favorite-game-show-celebrates-its-first-year-with-alexa)). **postmortem**
- **Audio pacing:** the "one-breath test" says if a line can't be said in one breath, cut it, and don't read menus aloud ([Alexa design guide](https://developer.amazon.com/fr/designing-for-voice/what-alexa-says/)). Radio writing rules: one idea per sentence, ~8–20 words, because a listener can't rewind ([IJNet](https://ijnet.org/en/resource/how-write-broadcast-news-stories)). **anecdote / practitioner consensus**
- **In-car family play:** a 6-week field study of an in-car kids' game found children want fun while parents want bonding, and listed short engagement and motion sickness as design challenges ([Mileys, RUNI](https://runi.ac.il/media/l12nxph0/in-car-game-design-for-children-child-vs-parent-perspective.pdf)). The nICE cooperative game includes the driver at their own ability level ([Augsburg](https://opus.bibliothek.uni-augsburg.de/opus4/files/50547/50547.pdf)). In a survey, 74% of adults remember enjoying group car games as kids vs 36% solo activities ([Kwik Fit](https://prnewswire.co.uk/news-releases/kwik-fit-research-shows-families-missing-out-as-they-turn-to-screens-on-summer-car-journeys-301882509.html), press survey). **data (qualitative) / anecdote**

## 3. New research beyond what we already have

- **Teasing effect:** across 7 studies, giving partial information first and resolving it afterwards was *more enjoyable* than giving everything at once, yet people *choose* to get everything at once when asked. One study used trivia ([Ruan, Hsee & Lu 2018, JMR](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/The-Teasing-Effect-An-Underappreciated-Benefit/9984380448802771)). **data.** Implication: a short built-up pause before the verdict beats an instant "Correct". Don't let user feedback ("faster please") talk you out of it without testing.
- **Suspense > surprise for audiences:** a formal model of suspense and surprise as entertainment ([Ely, Frankel & Kamenica 2015, JPE](https://www.wiwi.uni-bonn.de/kraehmer/Lehre/TopicsSS14/ElyEtAl-SurpriseSuspense.pdf)). In NBA viewership data, +1 SD of suspense means +2.5–2.9% viewers, while surprise has no measurable effect ([Kaplan & Zorrilla, AEA 2023](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2023/program/1348)). **data** (working paper). Implication: suspense comes from *something riding on the outcome*, such as a score race, a wager or a banked total. Surprising facts alone don't create it.
- **Uncertain rewards beat certain ones:** in a maths quiz, 10–11-year-olds preferred a coin flip for 2 or 0 points over a sure 1 point, and the preference *grew* during the quiz ([Howard-Jones & Demetriou 2009](https://www.td.org/insights/uncertainty-and-chance-may-improve-learning-in-learning-games)). A reward-prediction-error model predicted recall ([Howard-Jones et al. 2011](https://www.mrcbndu.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/pdf_files/Toward%20a%20Science%20of%20Learning%20Games.pdf)). **data.** Supports wagering and double-or-nothing.
- **Hypercorrection:** wrong answers given with high confidence are remembered best once corrected. Low-confidence correct guesses also surprise. Both come from a confidence/feedback mismatch ([Butterfield & Metcalfe 2001; review: "Understanding the Hypercorrection Effect", Duke](https://dukespace.lib.duke.edu/items/d6ab09ca-f55c-4efb-9d32-2788bc5838b2)). **data.** Implication: asking for confidence (a wager) makes the reveal hit harder.
- **Near-miss:** near-misses feel worse than clear misses but *raise the desire to play*, and only when the player had control ([Clark et al. 2009, Neuron](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2658737/)). **data**, but from gambling: use it ethically, e.g. "so close, it was 1969 not 1968" for estimation, never fake ones.
- **Social presence:** a co-located co-player adds to fun, challenge and perceived competence vs virtual or mediated play ([Gajadhar et al. 2008](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-540-88322-7_11)). **data**
- **Need satisfaction drives enjoyment:** autonomy, competence and relatedness independently predict enjoyment and future play ([Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski 2006](https://www.rochester.edu/warner/lida/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/02bfe513dd59366750000000.pdf)). **data.** Trubbo's single-player, no-choice loop is weak on autonomy and relatedness (inference).
- **Adaptive difficulty doesn't reliably add fun:** n=221, dynamic difficulty improved performance but made **no difference** to enjoyment or flow vs fixed or steadily rising difficulty ([Robb & Zhang 2022](https://actaludologica.com/performance-based-dynamic-difficulty-adjustment-and-player-experience-in-a-2d-digital-game-a-controlled-experiment/)). **data.** Implication: a *visible* climb (1% Club, Millionaire) is the lever, not hidden adaptation.

## 4. Levers: evidence and voice fit

| Lever | Evidence (strength) | Voice/hands-free fit |
|---|---|---|
| **Wager / double-or-nothing / confidence bet** | Howard-Jones (data), hypercorrection (data), Millionaire, Chase, Final Jeopardy (postmortem) | **High.** One spoken word ("double"/"safe"). Creates suspense without the screen. |
| **Visible escalation + bank/walk-away** | 1% Club ratings, Millionaire ladder, QuizUp double-points finale (postmortem); hidden DDA adds nothing (data) | **High.** The host announces the level, so the round arc lives in the audio. |
| **Lifelines** (50:50, hint, pass) | Millionaire, 1% Club pass, Trivia Crack coins, HQ Erasers (postmortem) | **High** for "hint"/"pass" by voice. 50:50 suits MCQ only. |
| **Co-located multiplayer / teams** (car, party) | Gajadhar (data), Volley, Kahoot, Jackbox, Heads Up (postmortem), Kwik Fit (survey) | **High** if turn-based with named players (no speaker identification needed). The driver plays by voice only. |
| **Host personality + humor** | YDKJ, HQ, Wait Wait, QI, Matchbox silly option (postmortem); repetition risk (anecdote) | **High.** Voice is the *only* channel, so the host *is* the product. Watch TTS cost and line repetition. |
| **Reveal as a moment** (tease pause, % who got it right, a surprising "why") | Teasing effect, suspense (data), 1% Club % framing (postmortem) | **High.** Pure audio. |
| **Format variety** (estimation/closest wins, which came first, higher/lower, odd one out, progressive clues) | Wits & Wagers, Only Connect, Pointless, Wait Wait (anecdote/postmortem) | **High** for estimation, which came first and true/false (short spoken answers). Medium for odd one out (needs 4 items held in memory). |
| **Daily ritual** (question of the day, streak) | Question of the Day, Jeopardy Alexa (postmortem); Duolingo split streak test: +3.3% D14 retention ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak)) (data) | **Medium.** Car trips are irregular, but a 1-question daily drive-time ritual fits. |
| **Speed scoring / countdown** | Kahoot, QuizUp (data/postmortem); stress downsides (data) | **Low.** Unsafe while driving, and STT latency makes timing unfair. |
| **Global leaderboards** | Matchbox (postmortem) | **Low** for fun in the moment. A local "family table" is better. |
| **Audio clips (songs)** | Volley Song Quiz, its top genre (postmortem) | **High** fit but blocked by licensing (see `ai-music-licensing-quiz.md`). |
| **Theme/narrative wrapper** | TMP murder host, Yes Sire (postmortem) | **Medium.** Fun for kids' packs, adds TTS length. |
| **Music bed under questions** | Kahoot (data) | **Medium.** Conflicts with the founder's minimal-audio rule; a subtle bed only in party mode could be tested. |

## 5. Top 5 levers for Trubbo (ranked)

1. **Stakes inside every round: "safe or double" wager + a visible climb.** Questions get harder across the round and the host announces it ("level 3 of 7"). Before each answer (or from Q4 on) the player says "double" or "safe", and the last question counts double. *Expected effect:* turns passive answering into a decision. Creates suspense (data: +2.5–2.9% NBA viewers per SD of suspense, uncertain rewards preferred) and makes reveals land harder (hypercorrection). *Size:* **medium** (scoring model + 2 voice commands in sk/cs/en + host lines; uses existing difficulty field).
2. **The reveal as a staged moment.** Tease pause → verdict → "only 23% of players got this" → one surprising sentence that passes the one-breath test. Silly-but-plausible fourth MCQ option. *Expected effect:* the payoff that makes the next question wanted (teasing effect, 1% Club framing, Matchbox). *Size:* **small–medium** (per-question correct-rate needs answer events aggregated server-side, likely from the #51 analytics table; the pause and copy are cheap).
3. **Car/party multiplayer, turn-based by name.** "Two players: Michal and Ema." Questions alternate, kids get easier ones, team mode for "everyone shouts". Ghost rule: whoever is behind gets a catch-up question (Trivia Murder Party). *Expected effect:* the biggest step change in fun per the evidence (co-located play data, Volley's core thesis, car-game survey). Directly targets the party/kids use cases. *Size:* **large** (session model for N players, scoring, voice turn routing, UI). Today it's post-MVP in `CONTEXT.md`; scope with the founder.
4. **A host with personality.** Short scripted reactions to streaks, near misses, a big wager won or lost, and confidently wrong answers (QI-style "klaxon" for popular misconceptions). One voice, consistent persona, a large line pool so jokes don't repeat. *Expected effect:* emotional texture, which is what YDKJ, HQ and Wait Wait sold. *Size:* **medium** (writing + pre-rendered and cached TTS clips to keep ElevenLabs cost flat; sk/cs/en copy review).
5. **Format variety within a round.** Mix in estimation ("closest wins", the near-miss line when close), "which came first", true/false streak bursts and progressive clues (fewer points per clue). *Expected effect:* breaks the monotony of identical Q&A beats. Estimation and progressive clues also give experts and novices both a chance (Wits & Wagers, 1% Club "anyone can play"). *Size:* **medium** per format (generation prompts + answer judging; estimation needs numeric parsing). Start with estimation and which-came-first (smallest).

*Deliberately not top 5:* speed scoring (unsafe while driving), hidden adaptive difficulty (no measured fun gain), global leaderboards (weak in the moment), cash prizes (HQ's cost trap), music bed (conflicts with the founder's minimal-audio rule). A daily drive-time question is a cheap retention add-on once the core loop is fun.

**Suggested validation:** paper-prototype levers 1 and 2 first (Volley's "one person plays Alexa" method, [Alexa Games Week](https://developer.amazon.com/en-US/blogs/alexa/alexa-skills-kit/2020/05/8-things-we-learned-from-alexa-games-week)). A founder plus one passenger reading a round aloud with wagers will show within one drive whether stakes fix "boring", before any code.
