# Reels: questions per reel, fixed hook line, A/B timing (2026-10-25)

Builds on `social-reels-hook-rate-2026-10-23.md` and `social-reels-hook-text-and-captions-2026-10-24.md` (not repeated). Grades: **[P]** platform/official · **[I]** industry/vendor data or blog · **[A]** anecdotal · **[R]** our reasoning. "Gap" = nothing found. Pages marked (snippet) were seen only in search summaries, not opened.

## TL;DR
- **No controlled data exists for 1 vs 3 vs 5 questions in a trivia reel.** Everything below is length data, vendor advice or reasoning. Our own A/B is the only real answer.
- **Recommend a 3-question "round" (~40-45 s) tested against the current 1-question reel (~25-28 s), not a replacement.** 5 questions is out: with ~10 s explanations it would run 70 s or more.
- **Reveal right after each question** (not all at the end). Full ~10 s explanation only for the strongest question; the other two get a 2-3 s one-line reason. Running tally "1/3, 2/3" is the built-in reason to stay.
- **Fixed hook line must be true for every question**, which rules out difficulty claims ("hard one", "most get this wrong"). Pick: **"The answer is better than the question."** (promises our strongest asset, the explanation, and keeps people to the end). Runner-up: **"Will you make it?"** (timer stakes). Table in section 2.
- **A/B timing:** a 0-follower account cannot use Trial Reels (threshold 200 or 1,000 followers, sources conflict). Test from day 1 by alternating arms on real posts (the cold start hits both arms equally); format first (weeks 1-3), hook line second (weeks 4-6), lock, then Trial Reels once eligible.

## 1. Questions per reel

### 1.1 Evidence

**Reading the conflict:** reach and median views favour 45-60 s, completion favours under 30 s. They measure different things (views of bigger accounts vs share who finish), so neither decides it for a 0-follower account. Both agree that over ~60 s is worse and that the first 3 s decide most drop-off. Gap: no figure for trivia, none for a 3-question vs 1-question comparison, none for SK/CZ audiences.

### 1.2 Trade-off [R]
- **1 question (25-28 s):** high completion, loops, cheap to produce, each reel gets the explanation in full. Weak spot: no reason to stay after the answer's tension is gone, and a strange question can be skipped in 1 s.
- **3 questions (40-45 s):** a score to protect ("1/3") gives a reason to stay past the first answer, and a miss on Q1 can still be redeemed. Cost: later drop-off risk, and the total looks longer than the Skip Rate window suggests, so Q1 must be winnable and fast.
- **5 questions:** needs 2-3 s per item to stay under 60 s, which kills the explanations (the part we think is the product). Not recommended.

### 1.3 Recommendation
- **Format R3 (3-question round), 40-45 s.** Q1 easy, Q2 medium, Q3 hardest and with the best explanation (open loop: the best part is last). Increasing difficulty is observed on quiz channels [6][A], not tested.
- **Per-question timer:** 4 s for Q1 and Q2, 5 s for Q3 (our current 5 s stays for the 1-question reel). Short timers are standard for Shorts [A, Typito] but too short for hard questions breeds frustration (prior doc).
- **Reveals after each question** [R]: the answer arriving 10+ s after its question kills the timer payoff and asks viewers to remember Q1; observed on big channels [6][A]. A reveal-at-end list also hides the explanations behind the longest wait, where drop-off is highest.
- **Explanations:** Q1 and Q2 get the answer plus one line (2-3 s); Q3 gets the full explanation (~10 s). Budget: 3 x (read 4 s + timer 4 s + answer 2 s) + ~8 s extra on Q3 + 2 s score card = ~42 s. Pick "the best" per round at generation time (the pipeline already scores surprise). Spare detail goes into the caption, not the video.
- **Score:** small tally chips at the top ("1/3 2/3"), final score card 1.5 s. No "score yourself" instruction needed; the tally is the cue.
- **Keep the 1-question reel alongside.** It is the control, the better loop, and the cheapest. Starting mix: format test below, then follow the data; if R3 wins on watch time per view and sends per reach, shift to ~3 R3 : 2 single per week.
- **Metric caution:** a 1-question reel will always show higher completion %. Compare formats on **average watch time (seconds) per view, Skip Rate, sends per reach, follows per 1,000 views**, not completion %.

## 2. Fixed hook line candidates
Rules applied: the line must be **true for every question** (so no "hard", "most people fail", any percentage), no dashes, no instructions (no "answer out loud", "pause"), at most ~6 words so it reads in under 2 s. Source of the principle: specificity beats generic (prior doc); curiosity gap and challenge framing are consensus advice [I], none tested on trivia. Hook/payoff mismatch is the main risk [I, async/socialk.it in prior doc], so each "why" names what must be delivered.

| # | EN | SK | CS | Why | Fits |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The answer is better than the question. | Odpoveď je lepšia ako otázka. | Odpověď je lepší než otázka. | Open loop to the explanation, our strongest asset; keeps viewers to the end. Payoff = explanation must stay good. | 1-q and multi-q |
| 2 | Will you make it? | Stihneš to? | Stihneš to? | Stakes = the timer; works for every question; short. Mild, not pushy. | both |
| 3 | Can you get it right? | Trafíš to? | Trefíš to? | Plain challenge framing, lowest risk, but the most generic (prior doc ranks generic weakest). Baseline line. | both |
| 4 | Bet you know this one. | Stavím sa, že toto vieš. | Vsadím se, že tohle víš. | Flatters, makes it feel winnable, lowers skip; a friendly dare. Mismatch on a very hard question is playful, not false. | 1-q best |
| 5 | Beat the monkey. | Porazíš opičku? | Porazíš opičku? | Game stakes plus mascot memory. Only honest if the monkey visibly "plays" (e.g. shows its own guess). A monkey-timer quiz channel exists already [6], so not unique. | both (needs mascot payoff) |
| 6 | Can you get 3 out of 3? | Trafíš všetky tri? | Trefíš všechny tři? | Score framing plus built-in reason to stay; the number is true by construction. | multi-q only |
| 7 | Three questions. Easy to hard. | Tri otázky. Od ľahkej po ťažkú. | Tři otázky. Od lehké po těžkou. | Sets the ramp, promises a hard finale (open loop), true by construction. Longer (6 words). | multi-q only |
| 8 | Today's question. | Dnešná otázka. | Dnešní otázka. | Series cue and recognisability; low curiosity. Honest only if posts are same-day fresh. | 1-q best |
| 9 | Question 37. | Otázka 37. | Otázka 37. | Numbered series (counter grows, signals a body of work). Friction: independent reels invite "where is 1?" (prior doc, untested). "of 100" commits us to an end. | both |

Pick: **#1 as primary** because it is the only one that gives a reason to stay through the explanation phase (the likely late-drop point, prior doc); **#2 as challenger**. If the multi-question format wins, add #6 or #7 as a second line only for those reels (two lines total, each fixed per format).
Lines to avoid: "Most people get this wrong", "You won't believe...", "Don't scroll" (untrue or pushy, and the pushy-sounding ones fail the founder's tone brief). Gap: no source tests fixed-line recognition on a 0-follower account; the YouTube/Instagram rules do not penalise a repeated opener (prior doc [P]).

## 3. A/B timing plan (~5 reels/week/account)

### 3.1 Facts
- **Trial Reels eligibility conflict [verify in app].** Mosseri, 14 Jul 2025: "all creators with at least 1,000 followers and an account set to public" [P-ish via news] [9][10]; other guides say 200 (professional) / 1,000 (personal) [I] [11]. Public account, eligible for recommendations. Either way a 0-follower account is **not eligible** now.
- Trial Reels show to non-followers only, first read ~24 h later, metrics listed: views, likes, comments, shares, avg watch time [10][12]. **Whether Skip Rate appears in Trial insights is unverified.** Limit 20 trials per day [I]. Kapwing's two tests: trial views 2.3x and ~1.0x the normal version; small n, no skip data [13].
- **Instagram Skip Rate** (Aug 2025) is in normal Reels insights; benchmarks are vendor-only (prior doc). **Shorts: "Stayed to watch"** (viewed vs swiped) is the official name for hook rate, in Studio > Content > Shorts [P] [14]; average view duration and % viewed are computed only on those who stayed.
- Typical starts [I, vidIQ]: median first Short ~1,362 views in 30 days; an individual Short reaches 1,000 views only ~57% of the time; best of the first 10 median ~8,800. Volume of posts buys breakthrough chances [15]. No equivalent figure found for Instagram.
- No native hook A/B tool for Shorts found; YouTube Studio's per-second retention graph is the instrument [I].
- **Sample size (our calculation, two-sided 5%, 80% power, two proportions):** detecting Skip Rate 50% vs 40% needs ~390 views per arm; 40% vs 30% ~360; a 5-point gap (50 vs 45, 40 vs 35) needs ~1,500 per arm. At ~1,000 views a reel, view count is not the limit: **reel-to-reel content variance is** (a good question beats any hook), so reels, not views, are the sample.

### 3.2 Plan
**Rule: interleave, don't run a "baseline period".** A cold start (small, noisy early distribution) hits both arms equally if posts alternate, so a separate baseline wastes 2 weeks. Exception: hook-line tests wait until format is chosen, so only one variable moves at a time.

| Weeks | Reels (5/wk) | Test | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | none | Setup | Confirm Skip Rate shows on a real post; spot-check captions on a phone; open ~6 big quiz accounts and note frame 1 (prior doc action). Decide eligibility of Trial Reels in-app. |
| 1-3 | ~16 | **T-A format: single (A) vs 3-question (B)**, 8 each, alternate days, same hook line (#1), same voice and look | Balance category and difficulty per arm; judge on median per arm, Skip Rate, avg watch seconds per view, sends per reach, follows per 1,000 views. Exclude a reel from the comparison until it has ~300 views or 5 days. |
| 4-6 | ~16 | **T-B hook line: #1 vs #2**, 8 each, on the winning format | Line stays fixed within each arm. After this, lock one line. |
| 7+ | 5/wk | Lock. Next: explanation captions (T3 prior doc), then question highlight | If the account has reached the Trial threshold, move small hook tests to **Trial Reels** (non-followers only, 24 h read, no follower contamination). |

**Decision rule (our rule of thumb, not a power result):** adopt B if its median Skip Rate is lower by ~8-10 points or more *and* the direction matches in at least 6 of the 8 arm pairs (pair by day); a gap under ~5 points is noise at this volume. If neither arm wins, keep the cheaper (single-question). Never pool EN with SK/CS, nor Instagram with Shorts; read Instagram on Skip Rate, Shorts on Stayed to watch. Expect a result per test in ~3 weeks per account; if the account is still under ~300 views per reel after the first 10 posts, extend to 10 reels per arm.
Guardrail: do not change the voice, music, length or posting time inside a test.
