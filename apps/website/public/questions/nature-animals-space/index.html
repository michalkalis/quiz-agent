Nature, animals and space quiz questions

Animals, plants, the human body and the planets. Nature surprises more often than you would think.

20 questions. One person reads, the others guess. Tap to see the answer.

  1. Which burrowing Australian marsupial is the only animal known to produce cube-shaped droppings?

    Show answer

    Wombat

    The cubes form thanks to uneven elasticity in the wombat's intestines, and the flat-sided pellets don't roll away, handy for marking territory on rocks and logs.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  2. Pigs love wallowing in mud, but not because they're dirty. They hardly do something that we humans rely on to cool down. What is it?

    Show answer

    Sweat

    Pigs have almost no working sweat glands, so wet mud cools them as it evaporates and also acts as sunscreen. The saying 'sweating like a pig' gets it completely backwards.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  3. Those little 'seeds' dotted over the outside of a strawberry aren't really seeds. Botanically, what is each one?

    Show answer

    A tiny fruit

    Each speck is an achene, a complete little fruit with its own seed inside. The juicy red part we eat is just the swollen base of the flower.

    Source: livescience.com

  4. A bullfighter's cape is red, but bulls can't even tell red from green. So what is it about the cape that actually makes the bull charge?

    Show answer

    Its movement

    Bulls charge at whatever is moving, and in tests they went for blue and white capes just as eagerly. The cape is red mostly for the audience, and because it hides bloodstains.

    Source: scienceabc.com

  5. A giraffe's neck can be over two metres long, yet it contains exactly the same number of neck vertebrae as yours. How many is that?

    Show answer

    Seven

    Almost all mammals, from mice to giraffes, have exactly seven neck vertebrae, a giraffe's are just stretched to around 25 centimetres each.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  6. An octopus's blue blood is pumped around its body by an unusual number of hearts. How many does it have?

    Show answer

    Three

    Two hearts push blood through the gills while a third serves the rest of the body, and that main heart actually stops beating when the octopus swims, which is why it usually prefers to crawl.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  7. One colourful crustacean strikes so fast that its punch creates bubbles of boiling water and has been known to crack aquarium glass. Name it.

    Show answer

    Mantis shrimp

    The mantis shrimp's club accelerates like a bullet, reaching around 80 km/h in a few thousandths of a second. The strike is so fast it creates cavitation bubbles that collapse with a flash of heat and light.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  8. The bird that lays the biggest egg compared to its own body, about a fifth of its weight, is the national symbol of which country?

    Show answer

    New Zealand

    It's the kiwi. Its egg is so large that the female eats more than usual for weeks beforehand, and an X-ray of her near laying time shows the egg filling most of her body.

    Source: guinnessworldrecords.com

  9. The world's largest single flower grows nearly a metre wide in the rainforests of Southeast Asia. To lure the flies that pollinate it, what does it smell like?

    Show answer

    Rotting meat

    Rafflesia is nicknamed the 'corpse flower' because it reeks of decaying flesh, which tricks carrion flies into visiting. Stranger still, it has no leaves, stem or roots at all and lives as a parasite inside a jungle vine.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  10. Apart from apes and monkeys, one animal has fingerprints so similar to ours that experts struggle to tell them apart, even under a microscope. It lives on eucalyptus leaves. Which animal is it?

    Show answer

    Koala

    Koalas evolved fingerprints completely independently of us, most likely to grip branches and handle leaves, and they're so close to human prints that people joke they could confuse a crime scene.

    Source: pbs.org

  11. A newborn Dalmatian puppy is missing the one feature the breed is famous for. What is it missing?

    Show answer

    Its spots

    Dalmatian puppies are born pure white, and their spots only start appearing after about two weeks. So a real litter would look nothing like the cartoon.

    Source: dogster.com

  12. Sea otters have a loose pocket of skin under each front leg. Besides food, what tool does an otter often keep in there?

    Show answer

    A rock

    Otters float on their backs, put the rock on their chest and smash clams and sea urchins against it, and many keep the same favourite rock for a long time.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  13. This single planet is so vast it could swallow all seven other planets of the solar system and still have space left over. Which planet is it?

    Show answer

    Jupiter

    Jupiter is the largest planet; the other seven planets would fit inside just 70 percent of its volume.

    Source: astronomy.com

  14. Which planet is so light for its size that it would float if you could find a bathtub big enough?

    Show answer

    Saturn

    Saturn is mostly hydrogen and helium, with an average density of only about 0.7 grams per cubic centimetre, lower than water.

    Source: coolcosmos.ipac.caltech.edu

  15. The word 'galaxy' comes from an Ancient Greek word for an everyday drink. Which drink?

    Show answer

    Milk

    The Greeks saw the pale band across the night sky as spilled milk, and called it 'galaxias' from 'gala', meaning milk. That is also why we still call our home galaxy the Milky Way.

    Source: merriam-webster.com

  16. From the ground a rainbow always looks like an arch, but pilots flying above the rain sometimes see it as what shape?

    Show answer

    A full circle

    A rainbow is really a ring of light centred on the spot directly opposite the sun, and on the ground the earth simply blocks the bottom half. So there is no end of the rainbow, and no pot of gold either.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  17. More than half of the 206 bones in an adult human are packed into just two paired body parts. Which two?

    Show answer

    Hands and feet

    Each hand has 27 bones and each foot has 26, so together they account for 106 of the 206, more than the rest of the body combined.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  18. Between a fifth and a third of people have an inherited reflex that makes them do something odd when they step out of a dark building into bright sunshine. What do they do?

    Show answer

    Sneeze

    It's called the photic sneeze reflex. Scientists think signals from the eyes' sudden response to light spill over onto the nerve that triggers sneezing.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  19. The smallest bone in the human body is shaped like a tiny stirrup. In which part of the body is it found?

    Show answer

    The ear

    The stapes in the middle ear is only about 3 millimetres long and passes sound vibrations from the eardrum to the inner ear.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org

  20. Astronauts returning from spacewalks report a surprising smell clinging to their suits, often likened to which seared food?

    Show answer

    Burnt steak

    Returning crews describe a metallic, seared-meat odour after spacewalks, thought to come from high-energy reactions on the suits; some compare it to burnt steak or welding fumes.

    Source: jameswebbdiscovery.com
