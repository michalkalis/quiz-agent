design variants · 2026-07-28

Issue #123 Track B — Pack credits + subscription state on Home

Home's plan card today speaks one language only: “N of 30 free questions left” for free users, “Unlimited questions” for subscribers. A user who bought a 100 Question Pack sees nothing about it, and a subscriber gets no confirmation that the €4.99 they pay is live — both facts already ship inside the same /usage payload (credit_balance, subscription_status) and are already rendered in Settings. Three approaches below, each shown across the five states that matter, using the real Theme.Hangs tokens and the real card geometry from HomeView.freePlanCard. Every variant keeps Track A's shipped loading treatment coherent — the slot never goes blank and never changes height between loading, loaded and failed.

Phone frames are 390 × 844 shown at 78%; the card-detail strips under each variant are actual size. Colour = the meaning of the money, consistently across all three variants.

variant a

One adaptive balance card

The card stays a single surface and a single tap target, but its headline becomes the number the user actually spends. Free users read their quota; credit holders read one combined balance with a two-tone track; subscribers read a word instead of a number. Nothing is added to Home — the existing card just learns to say more.

Decisions this variant makes

StatesSix distinct visuals: free · free+credits · active · active+credits · grace · expired. Loading and failed keep the shipped shapes.
Primary numberThe total spendable (free + credits) when both exist. The track becomes a two-tone spend meter, blue = monthly, purple = credits, drawn in burn order.
One card or splitStays one card — the founder's existing single tap target survives untouched.
Tap targetWhole card, destination is state-dependent: paywall (free / free+credits), manage subscription (active), App Store billing (grace).
Upgrade affordanceSame slot, different verb: “Upgrade” → “More” (has credits) → “Manage” (subscribed) → “Fix payment” (grace).
Grace / expiredHome warns. Grace turns the whole track amber — a lapsing subscriber is the one person who must act, and Settings is two taps away.
LoadingTrack A's spinner and “Loading your plan…” copy stay verbatim; the label, empty track and a skeleton meta line hold the card at its final height so nothing jumps.

Tradeoffs

  • Best glanceability at speed. One Anton number is readable at arm's length in a car mount; the current 13px sentence is not.
  • Zero new Home real estate — the card grows from ~64pt to ~112pt but stays one block, and the tap semantics never fork.
  • “112” conflates two currencies. The user cannot tell from the headline alone that 12 of them expire in three days; the legend row carries that burden.
  • Most state logic per pixel. Six layouts through one card means the height changes between states (subscriber card is shorter), so Home shifts slightly on state change.
  • Scales to future states by substitution, not addition — a trial, a family plan or a bonus-credit promo replaces the headline rather than joining it, so the card can never show two offers at once.
variant b

Stacked entitlement rows

One row per thing you own, hairline-separated inside the existing card, each row its own tap target. Rows appear as entitlements are acquired and vanish when they aren't there — the card is a ledger, not a headline. Structurally this is the same idiom as the Home config card and the Settings groups, so it needs no new component.

Decisions this variant makes

StatesEvery state is a composition of three row types (plan · credits · action), so nothing needs its own layout; grace adds a fourth, amber-tinted row.
Primary numberNo combined number. The monthly quota stays primary because it is the one that expires; credits read as a stated stock. The track keeps its current meaning — free quota only.
One card or splitSplit inside one card. This is the variant that directly answers the issue's “plain row + credits row” option.
Tap targetPer row, which resolves the two-meanings-one-surface problem: quota row → paywall, credits row → packs, action row → paywall, grace row → App Store billing.
Upgrade affordancePromoted to its own row with the price in it (“Upgrade to unlimited — €4.99/mo”), and simply removed once the user subscribes.
Grace / expiredHome warns, loudly — grace gets a tinted row above everything else; expired reverts the plan row and keeps the credits row, so a lapsed user still sees what they own.
LoadingUnchanged from Track A: exactly one row, spinner + “Loading your plan…”. This variant is the only one where the shipped placeholder is already pixel-correct.

Tradeoffs

  • Never ambiguous. Each currency keeps its own row, its own colour and its own destination — no arithmetic for the user, no shared tap target to disambiguate.
  • Cheapest to build and to test. Reuses the existing card + hairline idiom; each row is independently unit-testable, and Track A ships as-is.
  • Scales best. A trial, a bonus, a family seat = one more row; the rules stay “one row per entitlement”.
  • Tallest on Home. Free + credits is three rows (~150pt) and pushes the config card and the Start Quiz CTA down — the driving-first CTA is the thing this design most risks.
  • Lowest glanceability. All information is 13pt body text; there is no big number, so it is read, not glanced.
variant c

Compact badge strip

The plan card disappears. Entitlements become a row of small pills directly under the tagline — one pill per thing you own, plus a purple “+” to top up. Home gets ~70pt shorter and the Start Quiz CTA moves up, at the cost of dropping the card as a paywall surface.

Decisions this variant makes

StatesCollapses to two pill types (a balance pill and an action pill) recoloured per entitlement; grace and expired reuse the same shapes. Fewest distinct visuals of the three.
Primary numberNeither dominates — pills are peers, read left to right in burn order. The progress track is demoted to a 3pt fill line inside the free pill; there is no track for credits.
One card or splitNeither: the card is deleted. Home loses a card and gains ~70pt of vertical space for the CTA.
Tap targetPer pill (34pt tall — at the 44pt HIG minimum only if padded). Balance pills → paywall / packs, “+” → paywall.
Upgrade affordanceA dedicated ghost pill for free users, a purple “+” once they hold credits, and nothing at all once subscribed.
Grace / expiredHome warns via an amber pill pair, but with far less weight than variants A and B — arguably too little for a payment failure.
LoadingSkeleton pills at the same 34pt height, plus the spinner and Track A's copy in the first pill. Track A's behaviour is preserved; its card shape is not, so Track A's tests would need re-recording.

Tradeoffs

  • Least noise, shortest Home. The CTA sits higher, which matters more than anything else for a driver reaching for “Start Quiz”.
  • Scales sideways forever — any future entitlement is one more pill, and the strip can scroll horizontally without touching the layout.
  • Weakest paywall surface. Today the entire card is a proactive paywall entry (presentPaywall()); a ghost pill is a much smaller, easier-to-ignore target and likely costs conversions.
  • Smallest type on the screen. 10–14pt mono in a moving car is the worst of the three for glanceability, and 34pt pills are below the comfortable touch target.
  • Invalidates Track A's shape (not its logic): the shipped placeholder card becomes skeleton pills, so the Track A tests and the visual check get re-recorded.
Held constant across all three variants, so the pick is about layout only: blue = the monthly free quota (expires), purple = pack credits (never expire, spent after the free quota), pink = subscription and every paywall affordance, amber = grace/renewal failure, green = an active subscription. Copy reuses what already ships: “N of 30 free questions left”, “Unlimited questions”, “100 Question Pack · one-time purchase · never expires”, “Loading your plan…”, and the untouched failed-state “Couldn't load your plan · Retry”. All three read the two fields Home already receives — credit_balance and subscription_status — so whichever wins, Track B is presentation work with no new network call.

Still open for you to decide (the issue asks these explicitly): should a free user's pack credits appear on Home at all, or wait for the packs product story; and does Home warn about a lapsing subscription (all three variants above assume yes) or stay silent and leave it to Settings.
