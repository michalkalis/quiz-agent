Issue #125 Track B — Minimalist MCQ layout

Track A already landed the reveal gate: until the countdown expires the driver sees the stem alone, and the four option cards plus the listening pill appear only at answer time. What is left is the layout itself — after the reveal the option block takes the screen back, a long stem is cut mid-line, and nothing on screen says it continues. All three variants below share one rule: the stem gets a guaranteed floor, the option block lives in whatever is left, and the overflow always sits behind a visible affordance — never silently clipped. Everything is drawn 1 CSS px = 1 pt with the shipped tokens and component geometry (Anton / Inter / IBM Plex Mono, Theme.Hangs.Colors, AnswerOption 64pt / r16 / 1.5pt).

Worst-case copy in every frame is the shipped long-stem fixture Question.previewMCQLong (290 chars) extended to ~340 chars — the length the same question reaches once rendered in Slovak, which is what the founder actually had on screen. At 290 chars a slimmed layout very nearly fits on a 6.1-inch phone; 340 is where each strategy has to show its hand.

Variant AAnswer Grid

Halve the option block by putting the four choices in a 2×2 grid of letter tiles, and spend the reclaimed points on the stem — which gets the largest type of the three variants (Anton 34) in the answering phase, because at that moment the driver glances rather than reads. The pill stops being its own element: it becomes a full-width listening bar that also carries the mute control, so the audio strip disappears entirely once the timers have expired.

Answers to the Track B questions

HierarchyReading: stem is the only content. Answering: the stem still wins (34pt Anton against 16pt option labels) and does not move — the grid grows underneath it.
Stem floor360pt on 6.1-inch, 300pt on SE (≈43% and 45% of the screen). Past the floor: scroll only; type never drops below 30.
Overflow cueRight-edge rail with a proportional thumb + bottom fade + "SCROLL ↓" mono cue. Present whenever, and only when, content overflows.
Options2×2 grid of 88 × ~171pt tiles (76pt on SE). ~13 000pt² of target versus ~22 000 today — still far above the 44pt HIG minimum.
Listening pillBecomes a full-width 44pt listening bar that swallows the mute button; the audio strip is dropped after the reveal, since its timer chips have expired by then.
Chrome keptClose chip, progress bar, category + counter (merged into the top row), tap-to-replay with the speaker glyph pre-reveal only, mute, Skip. Dropped: settings gear (moves into the close sheet) and the separate meta row.

Trade-offs

  • Glanceability: best of the three — biggest stem type and the largest letters; A/B/C/D are read by position, not by scanning a list.
  • Touch safety: two columns add a horizontal aiming axis, which is harder in a moving car than a full-width row you can hit anywhere along x. The tiles are big, but the left/right split is a real cost.
  • Density: highest — frees 104pt against today and removes two chrome elements outright.
  • Extreme stems: the biggest type overflows first. At ~340 chars roughly half the sentence is below the fold on SE, so the scroll cue does real work here rather than covering an edge case.
  • Long option text: half-width tiles wrap after ~14 characters. "Bratislava" fits; "The Austro-Hungarian Empire" needs two lines and 88pt then becomes the binding constraint.

Variant BElastic Stem

Keep the shipped single-column list — the safest thing to hit while driving — and make everything else elastic: rows slim from 64 to 52pt, and the stem shrinks to fit before it scrolls (Anton 30 → 24 → 22 floor, exactly the minimumScaleFactor behaviour the component already has). The listening pill is dissolved entirely: the meta row that already sits above the stem turns pink and becomes the listening indicator, at zero extra height.

Answers to the Track B questions

HierarchyReading: stem dominant at 30. Answering: deliberately balanced — the stem keeps its size where it can, the options stay full width, and the only thing that changes state is the meta row turning pink.
Stem floor330pt on 6.1-inch, 220pt on SE. Order of defence: shrink type (30 → 24 → 22 hard floor) first, scroll second. Both, in that order.
Overflow cueSame rail + fade + "SCROLL ↓" as A, on a smaller viewport — so it appears more often, on more questions.
OptionsFull-width rows at 52pt (48 on SE) with 28pt badges. Above the 44pt minimum and still the full screen width to aim at.
Listening pillDissolved into the meta row: the category is replaced by a pink dot + "LISTENING — SAY A–D" for the duration of the answer window. Zero vertical cost.
Chrome keptEverything — close, gear, progress, meta row, replay glyph, timer chips, mute, full-width Skip. The least disruptive variant to build and to relearn.

Trade-offs

  • Touch safety: best of the three — a full-width row is the easiest target to hit without looking, and the vertical A→D order matches the order the options are read aloud.
  • Glanceability: weakest type in the squeeze (Anton 24 on SE, floor 22). Legibility at arm's length in a moving car is the open risk.
  • Density: highest — the most lines of stem per point of screen, and the listening state costs nothing at all.
  • Extreme stems: shrink-to-fit softens the problem rather than solving it. A 500-char stem ends up small and scrollable — the worst of both.
  • Cheapest to ship: no new components — a floor on the scroll region Track A already added, smaller row heights, a visible indicator, and one conditional in the meta row.

Variant CSplit Deck

Stop negotiating for space: split the screen at a fixed proportion. The stem owns the top 45% permanently, one pinned control strip is the divider (timers, listening state, mute and Skip in a single 44pt bar), and the options live in their own scrollable deck below it. The deck becomes the overflow victim instead of the stem — the exact inverse of today's bug — so the cards keep their shipped 64pt geometry and the stem type never changes between phases.

Answers to the Track B questions

HierarchyFixed by construction rather than by content: the stem always owns the top band, the options the bottom one. The reveal only moves the divider — no re-flow, no type change, so the eye never has to re-find the sentence.
Stem floor45% of the content area — 340pt on 6.1-inch, 271pt on SE — guaranteed regardless of option count or text length. Overflow always scrolls; type never shrinks.
Overflow cueRail + fade + a page counter ("1 of 2"), which also says how much is left. The one cue that survives a half-second glance.
OptionsUntouched 64pt full-width cards (80pt for the True/False variant), inside their own scroll deck. The safest possible target, at the price of not always seeing all four at once.
Listening pillFolded into the pinned strip, where it replaces the expired timer chips. One bar carries every transient control: timers → listening state, mute, Skip.
Chrome keptClose chip, 2pt edge progress, counter, tap-to-replay, mute, Skip (as a text button in the strip). Dropped: the gear, the category label, the standalone meta row, and the full-width Skip capsule — the most aggressive minimalist pass of the three.

Trade-offs

  • Glanceability: spatial constancy is its strongest asset — stem, controls and options sit in the same band on every device and in both phases, so muscle memory builds across questions.
  • Touch safety: the shipped 64pt full-width cards are kept verbatim; no target shrinks anywhere, on any device.
  • Density: lowest — a fixed split wastes room on short stems ("What is the largest planet?" leaves ~250pt of empty pane) unless the split is allowed to collapse for short text, which then costs exactly the constancy it was bought for.
  • Extreme stems: handled best (the pane is contractually guaranteed), but on SE it pushes the fourth option below the fold — fine for a voice-first flow where options are also read aloud, a regression for anyone tapping.
  • Most to build: two independent scroll regions, a pinned strip that changes owner mid-question, and the loss of the category label the meta row carries today.

Still open — needs a founder call

  • Is SE-class (375×667) really the floor? Every frame above assumes yes. If the real minimum is 6.1-inch, the option block gets ~120pt more room and B's type never has to shrink at all.
  • Should the pipeline carry a max stem length too? All three variants hold ~340 chars; none makes 600 chars pleasant. A generation-side budget (~300 chars) would let the chosen layout stop designing for the tail.
  • Slovak runs 15–20% longer than English for the same question — the floors above are sized on the Slovak-length render, not the English fixture. Worth confirming that is the right target.
  • Long option labels only bite Variant A (half-width tiles). If options can be long noun phrases, A needs a 2-line clamp rule before it is drawn in Pencil.
